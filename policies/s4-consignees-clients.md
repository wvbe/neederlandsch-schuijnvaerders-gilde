## Purpose

The Honest Manifest Order is the Gilde's standin' order (policy) on them as take delivery. It
holds that what the buyer is told of the goods be true, and that the papers of the goods
travel with them. It answers one matter that weighs in [the IRO register](../data/iros.csv):
provenance, a wrongly papered bale of sandalwood or a mislabelled cask reachin' a spice house
or a collector under the Gilde's own warrant (IRO-26).

S4 weighs only in part. The Gilde sells to houses and traders, not to households. The personal
safety of an end-user (IRO-27) was not found to be a material matter, for we deliver to houses
and agents, never to persons, and no such harm was found in either year. The rest the Standards
ask under S4, access to essential services, marketin' to vulnerable people and inclusion, is
likewise not material: we hold no account for a person and advertise to no household. This
order therefore binds the one thing that weighs, and holds the safe keepin' of cargo as a
commitment below.

The order reports its bad news first. In FY2025 **6 complaints** were made, all closed. One was
upheld, a cask of mace tainted by tar, replaced at EUR 6,200. One parcel of sandalwood was
delivered without its provenance papers and held 19 days.

## Scope

- **Activities.** The sale and carriage of spice, sandalwood and other goods to consignees; the
  goods carried or sold under commission for interdiction clients; and every sale of condemned
  prize goods at the Chamber of Port Royal.
- **Who takes delivery.** The 47 consignees (spice houses, collectors and island buyers), the 6
  interdiction clients, and the 214 registered buyers at the 6 prize auctions of FY2025.
- **Where.** All 6 chambers of the Gilde from FY2026, the Chamber of Vava'u included. FY2025
  figures cover the 5 chambers then open. No figure for Vava'u is held, and none is estimated.
- **Exclusions.** No person is served as a consumer, so no order is made for personal safety,
  essential services or marketin' to the vulnerable; the reason is the one given under Purpose.
  No other exclusion is granted.

## Accountability

The Captain-General is the highest office answerable for this order, and reports on it to De
Heeren Vijf and to the Kermis Council. The pursers of the hulls answer for the manifest, the
stowage and the papers of each parcel from lading to delivery. The Chamber of Port Royal auction
house answers for the origin statement and the sale. The clerk o' sales of each chamber answers
to the spice houses and collectors, and the Advocaat-Boekhouder, as registrar of commissions,
to the interdiction clients. Appeals from a complaint go to the Quartermaster-General, who is
also the counterweight officer.

## Commitments

1. **The manifest be true or the cask does not sail.** Every cask carries the chamber's device in
   wax and a tally number. The manifest names the island, the grower's mark and the weight, and
   the notary's count is taken against the manifest, not against our word.
2. **No substitution of cargo, ever.** Not a better cask for a worse, nor a nearer island for a
   farther. A cask short is reported short, and no false mark is set on any parcel.
3. **Provenance papers travel with the parcel.** No parcel of sandalwood, spice or any goods of a
   named grower or island is delivered without them. A parcel found without them is held until
   they are found, and no demurrage is charged for the hold.
4. **Origin statement at every auction.** Afore the biddin', the clerk reads out, under the bell,
   the 5 items of the origin statement: the mark's name and flag; the date and water of the
   seizure; the commission; the Prize Council's sentence and its date; and what of the lot is
   released to a consignee and so not sold. A buyer who asks is given a fair copy of the
   sentence in full. No bulk is broken afore adjudication (refusal condition 3).
5. **What we will not carry, we will not sell.** No ivory, rhino horn, pangolin, shark fin, live
   coral, reef stone or newly taken sea-turtle shell.
6. **The tortoiseshell is held, never traded.** The Port Royal warehouse holds 118 kg of old
   tortoiseshell of documented provenance, bought long afore the standin' order. It is counted at
   every muster, entered as held and not as stock, and not sold.
7. **Spice stowed clear of taint.** Spice is stowed away from tar, powder, hides and fish, never
   within two frames of them, and the magazine is behind a felt-curtained door.
8. **A complaint is heard and closed.** Any consignee, client or auction buyer may call the parley
   at the chamber that sold or carried, and appeal to the Quartermaster-General.
9. **Bad news first.** A missed mark, an upheld complaint or a false paper is reported plainly,
   ahead of the good news, with its cause and what will be done.

## Standards and shipmates

The order follows ESRS S4 and the Gilde's own standin' orders on the manifest. It follows no
outside provenance or chain-of-custody standard, and holds no certificate of one, so none is
claimed. The marks have not been validated by any outside body.

Whose interests were heard: the consignees and collectors, who raised the weight and the papers
at the parley; the 2 buyers who asked for the Prize Council's sentence in full, whose request
became the fair copy; the interdiction clients, whose agent sits at the table at each sale; and
the clerks and pursers who keep the manifests. No consignee or buyer sat on the Council that
set these marks, and the Gilde has not asked an outside body to speak for them.

## How it is made known

The order is posted at every chamber gate and at the Port Royal auction house, and carried in
each purser's chest. It is read to every new consignee by the clerk o' sales and to every
registered buyer on registerin'. Its commitments on the origin statement are read aloud at each
auction, under the bell. The public notice of a sale is posted at the chamber and the port 14
days before it. The order is given to every interdiction client with the registration of the
commission.

| A5 | A written register of complaints at each chamber, each dated when called and when closed, summed at the Kermis by the Advocaat-Boekhouder | Every chamber | FY2027 | Complaints closed within 30 days rise to all of them by 2029 (S4-T1) |

**Resources.** Every action is done with the pursers' and clerks' own hands and the ledgers they
already keep, and is charged to the chamber's ledger. None needs capital or operating expenditure
significant against the Gilde's turnover, so no amounts are given by action (ESRS 2 MDR-A,
paragraph 69). No action may break the articles' rule on gear:
"Nothing aboard or ashore that was not in use on a Dutch ship in the 1600s"
The registers and papers are of ink and paper.

All 5 marks were first set by this written order, so none has a FY2026 milestone: that year had
closed before the order was adopted. FY2026 closed on 2026-09-30, and its outcome is
`[UNCHARTED]` until the ledgers for it are closed and sworn at Middelburg. The first milestone is
FY2027, and FY2025, the first counted year, is the baseline of each mark. The Gilde did not hold
these counts for FY2024, so the cell is empty. S4-T1 has no FY2025 value: canon holds that all 6
complaints were closed, but not how many days each took, so the baseline is read from the
FY2026 register once it is sworn, and is `[UNCHARTED]` until then. S4-T3 reads 100 because the
statement was read at 6 of 6 auctions in FY2025. S4-T2, S4-T4 and S4-T5 are counts, and S4-T2
and S4-T4 are held at 0, 2050 included. All marks cover all chambers from FY2026; the Chamber of
Vava'u has no measured value yet, and its baseline is set in its first full year.

The marks stand against what happened in FY2025, and it was not clean. Of 6 complaints, 1 was
upheld and 1 parcel was delivered without its papers. The shrinkage on the 150-day passage was
honest, and still the manifest should have said so; that is why S4-T5 is a mark and not an
excuse. A mark is met only by a complaint not made, or a parcel not missed, in the year.

The 2030 and 2050 marks are proposed to the Quartermaster-General for canon beside the FY2025
values; until they enter it, the register holds them. Every 2050 mark equals the 2030 mark,
for the aim is no mistake, and a mark for 2050 is no easier than the one for 2030.

## Monitoring and review

- **By the quarter.** The purser reads the manifest page against the notary's count at every
  landin'. The clerk o' sales reads the complaint register of the chamber.
- **By the Kermis year.** The Advocaat-Boekhouder closes the year at 30 September, and the
  Captain-General reads each milestone against it at the Kermis Council. The reading is reported
  in the reckonin' o' the year (sustainability statement) under S4-5.
- **When a milestone is missed.** The Raad van Kapiteins reports the cause first, names the
  action that will recover it, and does not lower the mark. An upheld complaint of substitution
  or false mark goes to De Heeren Vijf at their next sitting.
- **Review.** The order is renewed at every Kermis Council, with the marks reset no later than
  the Kermis of 2030. The order was adopted by the Kermis Council, October 2026.
