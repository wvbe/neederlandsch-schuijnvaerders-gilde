## Purpose

The Open Petition Order is the Gilde's standin' order (policy) on its dealings with regulators,
port authorities and estates (political engagement and lobbying). It holds that the Gilde asks
of those who govern the ports and provinces we use only by petition, in its own name, in the
open, and never by money or by a hand sent to speak for it in the dark.

No matter that weighs in [the IRO register](../data/iros.csv) concerns political engagement:
the G1 rows of the register are IRO-28 (an ill-founded seizure), IRO-29 (gifts to harbourmasters)
and IRO-30 (the bond and the costs of a failed seizure). This order is therefore held as good
conduct, not for a material IRO. It sits close to IRO-29, for a gift to a political cause and
a gift to a harbourmaster fail the same test, and the Gilde would rather be seen to pass both.

Bad news first: the Gilde has no measured record to report against these marks beyond FY2025.
The count of petitions in FY2024 is `[UNCHARTED]`, for it is not held in canon and this order does not supply it. In FY2025 the Gilde made 3 petitions; the posting of each at the chamber gates was
not recorded, and the first counted year for that mark is therefore FY2027.

## Scope

- **Activities.** Every approach the Gilde makes to a regulator, port authority, provincial
  estate or other public body, on any matter, and every payment of any kind that might be
  taken as political support.
- **Where.** All 6 chambers of the Gilde from FY2026, the Chamber of Vava'u included, and every
  officer and hand when speakin' for the Gilde. Vava'u has lodged no petition of its own; no
  value is given for it.
- **Value chain.** Own operations only. What a chandler or client does in its own name is its
  own affair, but no hand of the Gilde may ask one to do on the Gilde's behalf what this order
  forbids the Gilde.
- **Exclusions.** None. The customary gifts to port officials are governed by Article 22 and
  [G1-3](../csrd/g1-conduct.md), not by this order, and may never be given to further a petition.

## Accountability

The Captain-General is the highest office answerable for this order. She or he signs every
petition for the Raad van Kapiteins, and the Quartermaster-General, as the crews' counterweight,
countersigns it. Neither signature stands without the other. The Advocaat-Boekhouder attests the
political-contributions tally before it is marked final. The Quartermaster-General reports on
the order to De Heeren Vijf at every sittin' and reads every milestone at the Kermis Council.

## Commitments

1. **No political money, of any kind.** No contribution, in EUR or in kind, to any party,
   candidate, campaign, committee or foundation. No subscription of which any part is political.
   No political arm. The Gilde gave EUR 0 in FY2025 and EUR 0 in FY2024, and holds it at EUR 0
   every year to 2050.
2. **Petitions only, in the open.** The Gilde asks of a public body by petition alone, in its own
   name, signed by the Captain-General and countersigned by the Quartermaster-General. A petition
   is lodged with the body, stands on the public record of the body, and is posted at every
   chamber gate within 30 days of lodgin'.
3. **No registered lobbying, no instructed representative.** The Gilde is in no transparency
   register and instructs no agent, advocate or friend to speak for it. Should the Council ever
   decide otherwise, the entry in a transparency register is made, and named in the reckonin' o'
   the year (sustainability statement), before any lobbying begins. Lobbying without that prior
   entry is forbidden and is counted in this order's targets.
4. **No officer holds public office.** None of the Raad van Kapiteins, De Heeren Vijf, the
   Harbourmasters or the masters may hold public office while he or she holds the Gilde's.
   An officer who is elected or appointed to public office declares it to the Quartermaster-General
   at once, takes no part in any petition on a matter before that office, and before the next
   sittin' of the Raad either lays down the public office or the Gilde's. The declaration, the
   recusal and the choice made are reported in the reckonin' o' the year. Where it is the
   Captain-General who is concerned, the Presiding Lord of De Heeren Vijf signs for the Raad;
   where it is the Quartermaster-General, the Presiding Lord countersigns.
5. **What we petition must match what we promise.** No petition asks of a body what another
   standin' order of the Gilde forbids. Before lodgin', the Quartermaster-General reads each
   petition against the orders and certifies that it matches.
6. **Bad news first.** A petition made without the countersignature, a lobbying act without its
   register entry, or any political payment is reported plainly, ahead of the good news.

## Standards and shipmates

This order follows ESRS G1-5, which asks for political influence and lobbying activities,
including the amount of political contributions and the registered lobbying of the undertaking.
No outside code of lobbying is adopted, and no outside body validates the marks.

Whose interests were heard: the hands, who vote the articles; the port authorities at Vlissingen
and Baltimore and the Zeeland provincial estates, to whom the Gilde petitioned in FY2025; and the
Quartermaster-General, whose countersignature is the Gilde's own check.

## How it is made known

Each petition is posted at every chamber gate for the folk o' the ports to read, and its entry on
the public record of the body is named in the reckonin' o' the year. This order is read aloud at
the Kermis parley, carried in each master's sea chest, and told to every officer on taking office,
together with the duty to declare any public office offered.

## Monitoring and review

- **By the petition.** The Advocaat-Boekhouder's clerk enters each petition in the ledger on the
  day it is lodged and again on the day it is posted at the last gate.
- **By the Kermis year.** The Quartermaster-General reads each milestone against the ledger at
  the Kermis Council. The reading is reported in the reckonin' o' the year under G1-5.
- **When a milestone is missed.** The Raad van Kapiteins reports the cause first, names what will
  be done, and does not lower the mark.
- **Review.** The order is reviewed at every Kermis Council; the marks are reset no later than the
  Kermis of 2030.
