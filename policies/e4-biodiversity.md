## Purpose

The Living Coasts Order is the Gilde's standin' order (policy) on the livin' coasts an' forests
(biodiversity and ecosystems). The awkward matters come first. A wooden fleet eats trees, and the
forest it eats is windward o' us, in other people's hands. Our careenin' hards lie on living
shore. The Port Royal warehouse still holds old tortoiseshell. Rats still came ashore from our hulls
in FY2025, 2 times against 4 the year before: fewer, but not none. Hull timber from a named and
replanted source stood at 96% in FY2025, four points short of the mark. And the Chamber of Vava'u
has no survey of its hard, so how sensitive its shore is stands `[UNCHARTED]` until it is walked
and signed.

The order answers four of the matters that weigh in [the IRO register](../data/iros.csv): the
oak and pine felled for hulls, masts and spars (IRO-11); the careenin' and ballast work beside
reef, mangrove and whale water (IRO-12); the rats, weed and ballast sand carried between
chambers (IRO-13); and the exotic materials taken from island forests and reefs (IRO-14).

We plant what we cut, buy from groves and not from forests, and shall prove that we careen on no
living reef. We buy no offset and no credit against any of it.

## Scope

- **Activities.** Careenin', ballast work, ship-buildin' and timber buying, the carriage and
  provenance of exotic materials, and the keeping of cats, rat guards and tallies aboard.
- **Where.** All 6 chambers of the Gilde from FY2026, the Chamber of Vava'u included, and all 25
  hulls of the fleet. In FY2025 the hards of the 5 chambers then reporting lay on a total of 3.7 ha,
  and all 5 were sites in or near biodiversity-sensitive areas. The hard at Vava'u has no measured
  area yet: `[UNCHARTED]` until its first full year.
- **Value chain.** Own operations (the hards, the hulls, the Vlissingen oak) and windward o' us
  (the Baltic and Norwegian oak, the managed teak groves near Ambon, and the exotic materials the
  chandlers and growers supply). Leeward o' us there is no land taken.
- **Exclusions.** None. No marks are set on timber consumed, which was 1,634 m3 in FY2025: a
  fleet of 25 wooden ships needs the timber it needs, and a mark that cut it would mean fewer hulls
  careened.

## Accountability

The Quartermaster-General is the highest office answerable for this order. She reports on it to De
Heeren Vijf at every sitting and reads every milestone against the ledgers at the Kermis Council.
The Harbourmaster of each chamber carries out the order at the hard: the careenin' record, the
rat guards, the ballast ground. The master carpenter of each yard carries it out in the timber: the
papers for each purchase and the planting at Vlissingen. The Advocaat-Boekhouder attests the tallies
before they are marked final.

## Commitments

1. **No careenin' on livin' reef.** None on living coral, none at Vlissingen in the seal breedin'
   months, none at Baltimore before the grey seals have whelped, none at Sainte-Marie in the whale
   months. The mangrove behind the Port Royal warehouses is kept, not cut.
2. **The refused-cargo list.** No ivory, rhino horn, pangolin, shark fin, live coral or reef stone,
   wild-taken timber of a listed species, nor newly taken sea-turtle shell, and the rest of the
   list the articles hold.
3. **Tortoiseshell: held, not traded.** The old stock in the Chamber of Port Royal warehouse, of
   documented provenance and bought long before the standin' order, stays under seal with its
   papers. Canon holds this as `true`. No new shell comes aboard, and the stock is neither sold nor
   given away.
4. **Plant what we cut.** The Vlissingen yard plants oak on the polder at a rate not below 2.0 ha a
   year, so that future keels come from the Gilde's own woods. As POL-E1 says, the planting is
   claimed against no carbon mark and counted as no removal.
5. **Groves, not forests.** Hull timber comes from a named and replanted source, or it is not
   bought. Teak comes from managed groves under the grower's mark.
6. **Cats, guards and smoke.** Every hull carries a cat and keeps her. Casks and sacks land on a
   rat guard, never on the beach. Hulls are smoked at the careen. Every rat traced ashore is logged,
   reported to the port, and reported in the reckonin' o' the year.
7. **Dry ballast only.** Stone and sand, laid on ground the Harbourmaster has marked, washed
   before layin', never reef stone. No ballast water is carried.
8. **No gross mark is met by purchase.** No offset, credit or bought restoration stands against any
   mark in this order. No action here breaks the articles' rule on gear:
   "Nothing aboard or ashore that was not in use on a Dutch ship in the 1600s".

## Standards and shipmates

The order follows ESRS E4. The marks have **not** been validated by any outside body, and none
claims alignment with a science-based target for nature. The Gilde has not reckoned its dependencies
on nature beyond the Port Royal mangrove (see E4-6 in the topic file); any figure put on that
would be a guess.

Whose interests were heard: the fisheries of Port Royal and Ambon, who asked that the Port Royal
bounds be re-marked 15 paces back from the reef edge and who sign each hard survey with the
chamber; the growers who take the Ambon chamber's contract and its nursery seedlings; the
Harbourmasters, who walk the hards; and the hands, who keep the cats.

## How it is made known

The order is read aloud at every Kermis parley and posted at every chamber gate. A summary of the
careenin' closures goes up at each hard before the season opens. It is given to every chandler and
grower who supplies oak, teak, pine or exotic materials, and no cargo is stowed that its papers do
not match.

## Monitoring and review

- **By the careen.** The Harbourmaster reads the rat tally, the careenin' record and the ballast
  ground after each hull is hauled.
- **By the Kermis year.** The Advocaat-Boekhouder closes the year at 30 September and the
  Quartermaster-General reads each milestone against it at the Kermis Council. The reading is
  reported in the reckonin' o' the year (sustainability statement) under E4-4.
- **When a milestone is missed.** The Raad van Kapiteins reports the cause first, names the action
  that will recover it, and does not lower the mark. A rat traced ashore or a lot of timber without
  papers is reported the same year, plainly.
- **Review.** The order is renewed at every Kermis Council; the marks are reset no later than the
  Kermis of 2030.
