# POL-S2 The Fitting-Out Order — value-chain workers policy

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |

| Standard term | Value-chain workers policy (ESRS S2-1), with its engagement (S2-2), remedy (S2-3), actions (S2-4) and targets (S2-5) |
| Undertaking | Neederlandsch Schuijnvaerders Gilde N.V. and every chamber under it |
| Adopted | The Sainte-Marie clause (Article 21) and the chandler's undertakin': long in force. This written order: Kermis Council, October 2026 |

## Purpose

The Fitting-Out Order is the Gilde's standin' order (policy) on the hands that fit us out
(workers in the value chain). It binds the Gilde to deal fairly with the people it does not
employ but could not sail without: the near 1,630 hands on the floors of the rope-walks,
sawmills, cask yards, victuallers, spice farms and diving boats, the dock labour of the ports o'
call, and the crews of the vessels the Letters of Marque Division stops.

The bad news comes first. In FY2025 two suppliers paid their hands late, and EUR 3,100 in wage
arrears had to be made good. Eleven seized-crew complaints were raised and EUR 11,200 was paid
to the crews. Dock labour at the ports o' call is the weakest link in our chain, and a seizure
falls hardest on those who work for a wage aboard the vessel taken. We measure only what a
Harbourmaster can see, and 13 complaints from a chain of that size are fewer than such a chain
has.

The order answers three of the matters that weigh in [the IRO register](../data/iros.csv):
conditions at the rope-walks, sawmills, sail lofts and victuallers windward o' us (IRO-21); the
crews o' seized vessels (IRO-22); and dock labour hired by the day, off the muster roll (IRO-23).

## Scope

- **Activities.** All buying done by the Gilde, and all seizures made under a commissie by the
  Letters of Marque Division.
- **Where.** All 6 chambers from FY2026, the Chamber of Vava'u included, which opened in October
  2025. Its suppliers and the hands on their floors are `[UNCHARTED]` until the Harbourmaster
  of Vava'u has mustered a first full year; no figure is supplied for them by estimate.
- **Who.** The hands of the suppliers in the chandlers' book (the register of suppliers whose hands
  are mustered); dock labour hired at the ports o' call; and the crews of seized vessels. The sail
  loft at Ambon and the rope-walk at Vlissingen are the Gilde's own, and their hands are reported
  under S1, not here.
- **The count of suppliers.** The 63 suppliers on the chandlers' book are read as the subset of the
  96 suppliers of the Gilde whose hands are mustered.
- **Exclusions.** None. No supplier, seizure or season is exempt.

## Accountability

The Quartermaster-General is the highest office answerable for this order. She is the crews'
counterweight to the Raad, reports on it to De Heeren Vijf at every sitting, and hears every appeal
from the parley. The Harbourmaster of each chamber walks the suppliers' floors and keeps the hearing
book; the boardin' officer carries the written guarantees across the rail and answers for them
to the Harbourmaster of the chamber the prize is brought into. The Advocaat-Boekhouder attests
the tallies before they are marked final.

## Commitments

1. **No forced labour, no child labour.** The Sainte-Marie clause, Article 21: the Gilde deals in no
   person, holds none to labour and carries none against their will. Pressin' is abolished and is
   reported as a past wrong. Every hand aboard and on a supplier's floor is grown, and age is
   attested at the muster.
2. **The chandler's undertakin'.** No supplier enters the chandlers' book without signin' that
   their hands are grown, free to leave, paid in coin and not in kind, and open to the
   Harbourmaster's muster without notice. A supplier refused at one chamber is not bought from at
   another.
3. **The rope-walk and sawmill code.** Every hemp and timber supplier signs a written code on age,
   pay, hours and freedom to leave.
4. **The seized crew's guarantees.** Papers examined and persons never seized; no pressin' and no
   hostage; food, water and the surgeon; landin' at the nearest port with passage home at the
   client's cost. The 8 refusal conditions in [the Division's operations](../lore/operations.md)
   bind every boardin', and no client may buy an exception.
5. **The parley is open to any person.** Any person ashore in any port we use, and any seized crew
   through the Harbourmaster of the chamber, may call it at no cost. No person is docked or refused
   work for callin' it.
6. **A fair price to the grower.** The Ambon spice price is set with the growers' own season council,
   never at Vlissingen, and is held at or above the premium reported for FY2025.
7. **Payment.** When a supplier is paid, and in what coin, is the business of The Thirty Days Order
   (POL-G1-2) and is not restated here.
8. **Gear.** No action in this order breaks the articles' rule on gear:
   "Nothing aboard or ashore that was not in use on a Dutch ship in the 1600s".
9. **Bad news first.** A missed milestone is reported plainly, ahead of the good news, with its cause
   and what will be done about it.

## Standards and shipmates

This order follows the ESRS S2 requirements and the Gilde's own articles. It has **not** been
validated or audited by any outside labour body, and no outside code is claimed. No measure of a
living income has been taken for any grower; the Gilde reports the premium it pays and does not
claim that it is a living income.

Whose interests were heard: the Harbourmasters, who walk the floors and costed the visits; the
growers' season council at Ambon, which sets the spice price; and the gang spokesmen at the ports
o' call. The seized crews were not asked. A crew stopped at sea cannot say no, and the Gilde does
not consider the consent of those aboard obtained or capable of bein' sought. What it offers is
mitigation: a written procedure they may test, a record they may read, a parley they may call
after, and a client who pays when the Gilde was wrong.

## How it is made known

The order is posted at every chamber gate and read aloud at the Kermis parley. It is handed to
every supplier with the chandler's undertakin' to sign, carried in the boardin' officer's written
orders, and told to the growers at the season council at Ambon. A seized crew is told of the
parley by the boardin' officer before the vessel is released or brought in.

**Resources.** Every action is done with the Harbourmasters' own time and the chambers' ledgers. None
needs capital expenditure or operating expenditure that is significant against the Gilde's
turnover, so no amounts are given by action (ESRS 2 MDR-A, paragraph 69). The cost of the premium
and of arrears is charged to the chamber's ledger.

## Monitoring and review

- **By the visit.** The Harbourmaster enters every muster in the hearing book and reads it with the
  suppliers' payment tally. A finding of a hand under age or held against their will goes to the
  Quartermaster-General the same week.
- **By the quarter.** The parley book is read, and any complaint older than the days set in S2-T4 is
  reported to the Quartermaster-General.
- **By the Kermis year.** The Advocaat-Boekhouder closes the year at 30 September and the
  Quartermaster-General reads each milestone against it at the Kermis Council. The reading is
  reported in the reckonin' o' the year (sustainability statement) under S2-5.
- **When a milestone is missed.** The Raad van Kapiteins reports the cause first, names the action
  that will recover it, and does not lower the mark. A supplier that will not sign the code, or that
  is found with a hand under age or held against their will, leaves the chandlers' book.
- **Review.** The order is renewed at every Kermis Council; the marks are reset no later than the
  Kermis of 2030.
