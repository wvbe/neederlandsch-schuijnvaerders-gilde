## Purpose

The Articles and the Parley is the Gilde's standin' order (policy) on how the hands and the
Gilde deal with one another: the articles as the one instrument that binds both, the ships'
councils, the election and removal of officers, the vote of all hands at the Kermis, and the
parley (grievance mechanism) where a hand may be heard and, if the chamber rules against her,
heard again. It also states plainly what the Gilde lacks: a representative body from outside
its own walls.

It answers one matter that weighs in [the IRO register](../data/iros.csv): the strongest
positive impact we have on our own people, the one hand one vote, the parley and the
protections that go with them (IRO-20). It guards the remedy for the matter we have already
mended, forced recruitment, which the articles bar for ever (IRO-19). Hurts (IRO-17) and the
floor payment and the adequate wage (IRO-18) are the business of the workforce file; this order
neither restates nor re-targets them.

We say the weakness first. The parley is ours, kept by us and judged by us. We have not
measured whether hands trust it, and no one outside the Gilde sits at it.

## Scope

- **Who.** Every enrolled hand, at sea and ashore, and every officer, as the articles bind
  them all. The parley is open as well to any person ashore in a port we use.
- **Where.** All 6 chambers of the Gilde from FY2026, the Chamber of Vava'u included, and all
  hulls of the fleet. The first full year of Vava'u fixes its baseline; until then its cells
  read `[UNCHARTED]`.
- **Value chain.** Own operations only. The crews of seized vessels and the day labour at the
  hards are not enrolled hands; they are heard under the value-chain orders, not here.
- **Exclusions.** None. No hull, chamber or rating is outside the articles.
- **What stays elsewhere.** Health and safety, pay, diversity and training are held in
  [the S1 workforce file](../csrd/s1-workforce.md) and its marks (S1-5) are not re-set here:
  recordable hurts per 1,000 hands stood at 20.5 in FY2025 against a mark of 12.0 for 2030;
  women were 42.2% of all hands against 45.0%; the floor payment reached 100% of enrolled hands.

## Accountability

The Quartermaster-General is the highest office answerable for this order. She is elected by
all hands, sits as their counterweight, holds the parley book, and hears the parley on appeal.
Day to day it is carried out by the ships' councils, which elect and may remove a hull's
captain and send their minute to her, and by the quartermaster of each chamber, who keeps the
chamber's parley and reads the articles at signing. The Advocaat-Boekhouder attests the tallies
before they are marked final. Because the Quartermaster-General hears the appeal against her
own chambers, she reports on the parley to De Heeren Vijf sitting as the Muster, where she is
heard again alone with no captain in the room.

## Commitments

1. **One instrument, read to every hand.** Every hand signs or affirms the articles before a
   first watch is stood (Article 1), hears them read at signing, and hears them read again at
   the close of each Kermis year. A hand who cannot write makes a mark before two witnesses, and
   the mark binds the Gilde as fast as a signature binds the hand.
2. **The articles are changed only by the hands.** Amendment is by vote of all hands, one hand
   one vote, on a motion carried in the Raad and approved by De Heeren Vijf (Article 2). The
   date of every amendment stays in the margin of the Vlissingen copy and is never struck out.
3. **The hands elect and the hands remove.** A ship's company elects her captain and may put her
   out at any time save in chase or in battle (Article 11). All hands elect the
   Quartermaster-General (Article 12). Neither the Raad nor the Captain-General may set aside a
   vote of a ship's company in her own ship's business (Article 13).
4. **The parley is open and protected.** Any hand, and any person ashore in a port we use, may
   call it. The chamber hears it first and the Quartermaster-General on appeal; it is entered
   in the parley book with its outcome; and no hand is turned out of a ship, docked or shifted
   for calling it (Article 16).
5. **Every sanction is heard.** A fine, loss of share or dismissal follows a hearing with the
   hand present and the finding written, and each is appealable (Article 15). No hand is
   flogged, cut or struck; marooning is abolished (Article 14).
6. **No forced labour, no child labour.** Every hand is an adult. Pressing is abolished and
   reported as the past wrong it is; any hand may leave the Gilde at a voyage's end with what is
   owed (Article 21).
7. **Hearing outside the Gilde.** The Gilde will seek a seafarers' representative from outside
   its own walls to observe the parley, as set out under the actions below, and will report each
   step and each refusal plainly.
8. **The old gear.** No part of this order is carried out by gear that breaks the articles'
   rule: "Nothing aboard or ashore that was not in use on a Dutch ship in the 1600s".
   Ballots, minutes, the parley book and the muster roll are written by hand on paper.
9. **Bad news first.** A missed milestone, an upheld complaint of retaliation or a case closed
   late is reported ahead of the good news, with its cause.

## Standards and shipmates

The articles rest on the eight ILO core conventions on forced labour, child labour, equal
remuneration, discrimination and the freedoms to organize and bargain collectively (29, 105,
138, 182, 100, 111, 87, 98), as set out in S1-1. The grievance mechanism is read against the
effectiveness criteria of the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights. No outside
body has validated either reading, and the parley has not been tested against those criteria by
anyone but the Gilde.

Whose interests were heard in setting these marks: the hands, at the Kermis parley, where the
floor payment of FY2026 was raised on their motion; the ships' councils, through their minutes;
the chamber quartermasters, who keep the parley; and the Harbourmasters, who hear the first
complaint. No representative of the hands from outside the Gilde could be asked, since there is
none. That absence is the reason for target S1-T5.

## How it is made known

The articles are read aloud at the signing by each new hand and at the close of each Kermis
year, in every chamber and aboard every hull. A copy of the articles stands at every chamber
gate and in each master's sea chest. This written order is read at the Kermis parley, posted at
each chamber gate, and given to every ships' council. The parley is explained to a new hand at
signing, with the name of the chamber quartermaster who keeps it and the Article 16 clause on
the caller's protection. For hands who cannot read, it is read to them.

**Resources.** The actions are carried out by the chamber quartermasters, the Quartermaster-
General's clerk and the ships' councils, at their existing posts and from the chamber's own
ledger. No capital or operating expenditure significant against the Gilde's turnover is needed,
so no amounts are given by action (ESRS 2 MDR-A, paragraph 69). The observer of A7 is
unpaid by the Gilde beyond victuals and berth while in the chamber; whether a fee ought to be
paid is a matter the Council has not yet put to the hands.

The figures in the table are the marks we steer for. All five were first set by this written
order in October 2026. FY2026 had closed on 2026-09-30, so they carry no FY2026 milestone; the
first is FY2027, and the cell reads `—`. The outcome of FY2026 is `[UNCHARTED]` until the
Advocaat-Boekhouder has closed and sworn the ledgers for it. S1-T3 and S1-T5 were not
counted in FY2024, so they have no base.

**What the baselines are, and what they are not.** The cases-closed baseline is tallied from
the parley book: of the 31 parleys called in FY2025, 27 were closed inside the 28-day closure window (14 days
for the chamber's hearing and 14 for an appeal to the Quartermaster-General), 2 were closed after it and 2 were still open at the year's end;
of the 27 called in FY2024, 23 were closed inside the window. The book did not record the
window as a count before, so these baselines are a first reckoning, not a sworn series. The
turnout baseline is likewise a first count, taken from the muster roll marked at each Kermis
parley. It covers the 5 chambers of FY2025. Vava'u is first counted in FY2026; its baseline and
its shore headcount are `[UNCHARTED]`.

**A mark we did not set.** The Gilde has set no mark for an external representative body with
a standing at the table, and gives its reasons. The observer of S1-T5 has no vote and no power
to alter the articles; that is deliberate, for the articles are changed only by the vote of all
hands. Whether an outside seafarers' body should be recognised in the articles is a question
for the hands, not one the Council may settle for them, and the Council will put it to the
hands at the Kermis of 2030, once the observers have sat at every chamber for a year. Until
then S1-8's 100% stands, with the qualification set out there.

For reference, the parley in FY2025 heard 31 complaints against 27 in FY2024; 22 were upheld in
whole or in part, 7 were not and 2 were open at the year's end. Of them 5 were discrimination
incidents, including harassment, and none met the threshold of a severe human rights impact.
Complaints of retaliation numbered 0, and no hand has been found turned out for calling the
parley. Collective bargaining coverage is 100%, with the honest qualification in S1-8: the
instrument is real, written and enforceable, and there is no external representative body. No
metric in this order has been checked by an outside body beyond the assurance of the
sustainability statement.

## Monitoring and review

- **By the month.** Each chamber quartermaster reads the tally of the parley book at her
  chamber's board, with the cases past the window named.
- **By the Kermis year.** The Advocaat-Boekhouder closes the year at 30 September and the
  Quartermaster-General reads each milestone against the book and the roll at the Kermis
  Council. The reading is reported in the reckonin' o' the year (sustainability statement) under
  S1-3 and S1-8.
- **When a milestone is missed.** The Quartermaster-General reports the cause first, names the
  action that will recover it, and does not lower the mark. A finding of retaliation is
  reported first of all, with the name of the office held by the person found, and is dealt with
  under Article 15.
- **Review.** The order is renewed at every Kermis Council; the marks are reset no later than
  the Kermis of 2030, when the hands are asked whether an external body should be recognised.
