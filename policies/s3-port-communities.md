| Field | Value |

## Purpose

The Good Neighbour Order is the Gilde's standin' order (policy) on the folk o' the ports
(affected communities): the towns round our chambers, the island groups on the PAC-1 run, and
the growers' councils o' the Moluccas. It sets how we ask leave afore we anchor, water or lease,
how we hear the port, and how we make good what we have cost it.

We begin with what is wrong. In FY2025 the Gilde paid 23 port claims, EUR 61,500 in all. Of these
9 were for tar smoke at Vlissingen, the most of any chamber, and 2 of the 9 were paid only in part,
the smoke bein' shared with a kiln not ours. The hearings fell short of the mark: Baltimore held 3
port hearin's where the other four chambers held 4, so the Gilde held 19 and not 20. And one prize
taken in FY2025 stopped the trade of a trader ashore at Port Royal who was no party to the
commission.

The order answers two of the matters that weigh in [the IRO register](../data/iros.csv): what a
chamber costs its port (IRO-24), namely tar smoke at Vlissingen, moorin's at Baltimore, powder
days at Port Royal, water and market at Sainte-Marie and the waterfront at Ambon; and what a
chamber gives its port (IRO-25), which this order means to keep.

## Scope

- **Activities.** Every anchorage, watering, lease, landin' and sale made by the Gilde or on its
  account, and every prize taken under a commissie, so far as a third party ashore is touched.
- **Where.** All 6 chambers of the Gilde from FY2026, the Chamber of Vava'u at Neiafu included;
  the island groups of the PAC-1 run (the Bismarck Archipelago, the Solomon Islands, the Caroline
  Islands and Fiji); the growers' councils o' Banda and Ternate; and the village o' Ambodifotatra,
  from whom Sainte-Marie rents its water.
- **Value chain.** Own operations at the ports, and the communities leeward and windward o' them
  where our hulls call. Communities around the yards o' the chandlers are for the Fitting-Out Order.
- **Exclusions.** None. No chamber, island group or season is exempt.
- **Vava'u.** The chamber opened after the reported year and has no measured value. Its figures
  are `[UNCHARTED]` until its first full year is counted; none is supplied by estimate.

## Accountability

The Captain-General answers for this order. He presides at the Raad van Kapiteins, reports on it
to De Heeren Vijf at every sittin', and reads each milestone against the ledgers at the Kermis
Council. The Harbourmaster of each chamber carries it out: calls the port hearin', seeks consent,
receives the claim, and signs the closin' of it. Until De Heeren Vijf appoint a Harbourmaster at
Vava'u, the Harbourmaster of Ambon holds that duty. The Quartermaster-General pays port claims
from the hardship chest, and the Advocaat-Boekhouder attests the tallies.

## Commitments

1. **Hire from the port first**, and teach the trade: the Sainte-Marie ropewalk, the Ambon sail
   loft, the Vlissingen yard apprenticeship.
2. **Nothin' taken from a community without a written instrument it can read**: the water lease
   at Ambodifotatra, the waterfront lease at Ambon, the regatta-week undertakin' at Baltimore,
   the powder days given the Port Royal fishermen.
3. **Consent afore anchor.** Where we anchor, water or lease among the island communities o' the
   Pacific run, the Moluccas and Sainte-Marie, the Gilde seeks free, prior and informed consent
   (FPIC) through the community's own council or named speakers, afore the first cask is filled.
   Consent is recorded in the master's log.
4. **A refusal is honoured.** A refusal ends the matter for the season. The hull goes elsewhere
   and does not press again.
5. **No lease, no water.** Where a lease lapses the casks stay empty until it is renewed.
6. **Every claim heard and answered.** The parley is open to any person ashore in any port we
   use; a port claim is paid from the hardship chest and closed within the days set in S3-T3.
7. **A prize touches no stranger unheard.** Where a prize carries goods consigned to a third
   party, the chamber's Harbourmaster is told before the cargo is sealed, and the trade lost is
   made good as a port claim, whichever way the adjudication goes.
8. **Bad news first.** A missed milestone is reported plainly, ahead of the good news, with its
   cause and what will be done about it.
9. **The articles' rule on gear.** "Nothing aboard or ashore that was not in use on a Dutch ship in the 1600s".
   No action in this order is carried out with anythin' else.

## Standards and shipmates

The order follows ESRS S3 and holds FPIC as the Gilde's own rule on the island communities, in
the manner o' the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples. The marks
have **not** been validated by any outside body, and the Gilde does not claim alignment with a
named instrument beyond the wording above.

Whose interests were heard: the port hearin's of FY2025, where the claims were first named; the
village council o' Ambodifotatra, whose water lease was restated; the Pacific island councils,
who refused one anchorage; the growers' councils o' Banda and Ternate; the Harbourmasters, who
costed the work; and the hands, who voted the Vava'u chamber at the Kermis Council.

## How it is made known

Each port hearin' is announced a week ahead and minuted, with the chamber's own figures read out.
This written order is posted at every chamber gate, read at the first hearin' of each Kermis year,
and carried in each master's sea chest. At Neiafu the papers are posted in Tongan and English both.
Every instrument o' lease is given the community in a tongue it reads.

**Resources.** Every action is done with the chambers' own hands and the Harbourmasters' time, and is
paid from the chamber ledger or the hardship chest. None needs capital or operating expenditure
that is significant against the Gilde's turnover, so no amounts are given by action (ESRS 2 MDR-A,
paragraph 69). Port claims paid in FY2025 were EUR 61,500; that sum is a payment, not a cost of an action.

The figures are the marks we steer for. FY2026 closed on 2026-09-30; its outcome is `[UNCHARTED]`

the 4 of 4 island groups of the Pacific run consulted, and the 1 refusal of FY2025, in the
Caroline Islands, which was honoured.

**What the figures leave out.** S3-T2 counts the fewest hearin's held by any chamber, so a chamber
that falls short shows. FY2025's 3 is Baltimore's. The Vava'u count is `[UNCHARTED]`; the
chamber enters the count from FY2027. S3-T3's FY2025 value is `[UNCHARTED]`: every claim was closed
within the year, but the days were not counted until A5. The mark of 60 days was never tested against a
measured result, and the FY2026 milestone of 90 is the Raad's own allowance for a first year of counting.

**FPIC.** In FY2025 consent was sought 7 times: once with the council o' Ambodifotatra on the water
lease, 4 times on the Pacific run, and twice with the growers' councils o' Banda and Ternate.
6 ended in consent on terms and 1 in refusal. A mark of 100 % means a place is not entered
without the asking; it does not mean consent is always given.

**Climate and tar.** The Vlissingen tar-smoke marks (S3-T1) depend on the kiln work in POL-E2, the
Clean Wake Order. If the hoods or the tar mark there slip, the claims here will not fall.

Third-party trade stopped by a prize (1 case in FY2025) has actions (A8) but **no measurable target yet**.
The Council will set one at the Kermis of 2027, once a full year of the claims register shows how often it happens.

## Monitoring and review

- **By the quarter.** The Harbourmaster reads the claims register and the hearin' minutes, and
  reports any claim past its days to the Captain-General.
- **By the Kermis year.** The Advocaat-Boekhouder closes the year at 30 September, and the
  Captain-General reads each milestone against it at the Kermis Council. The reading is reported
  in the reckonin' o' the year (sustainability statement) under S3-5.
- **When a milestone is missed.** The Raad van Kapiteins reports the cause first, names the action
  that will recover it, and does not lower the mark. A claim that is shared with a kiln not ours
  is still counted.
- **Review.** The order is renewed at every Kermis Council; the marks are reset no later than the
  Kermis of 2030.
