# POL-E5 The Make-Do Order — resource use and circular economy policy

| Field | Value |

## Purpose

The Make-Do Order is the Gilde's standin' order (policy) on makin' do an' makin' over (resource
use and circular economy). It holds the salvage articles in writin' and sets out how far we shall
carry them: how much of what comes in is made over rather than bought, how much of a broken hull
goes back into the fleet, and how little of what is left is ever sent to landfill.

It answers two of the matters that weigh in [the IRO register](../data/iros.csv): the positive
impact of a wooden ship that is very nearly made over whole (IRO-15), and the opportunity in
salvage, which was always cheaper than purchase (IRO-16).

The bad news comes first. Only 11.2% of what the Gilde took in during FY2025 was reused or
salvaged, up from 10.4% in FY2024, and 1.3% of our waste still went to landfill, 4.6 t in all,
of which 1.4 t was hazardous. The 2030 marks ask for more than the yards have yet shown, and the
FY2026 outcome against them is `[UNCHARTED]` until the ledgers are sworn. The largest part of
the shortfall is not neglect. **Provisions are 44.2% of the purchased inflow, and food cannot be
made over.** That is why this order keeps two measures of the reused share: one over all material
input, and one with the provisions set aside, which shows how well the yards do with what can be
made over.

## Scope

- **Activities.** All careenin', ship-buildin', rope-walkin', sail-makin', coopery, victuallin'
  and ship-breakin' done by the Gilde or on its account, and the disposal of every waste it makes.
- **Where.** All 6 chambers of the Gilde from FY2026, the Chamber of Vava'u included, and all
  25 hulls of the fleet. Vava'u has no measured value for any mark in this order. Its baseline is
  set in its first full year and is `[UNCHARTED]` until then; no value is supplied by estimate.
- **Value chain.** Own operations, and windward o' us the chandlers who supply timber, hemp,
  canvas, iron, tar and provisions. Leeward o' us the Gilde sells carriage, not a product, so
  nothin' is made for a buyer to throw away.
- **Exclusions.** None. We hold no certificate of sustainable sourcin' and claim none.

## Accountability

The Quartermaster-General keeps the order as she keeps the common chest. She is the highest
office answerable for it, reports on it to De Heeren Vijf at every sitting, and reads every
milestone against the ledgers at the Kermis Council. The Harbourmasters and the master carpenters
carry it out: the Harbourmaster of each chamber for the yard's books and the hard, the master
carpenter for the timber, the cooper's book and the rope tally. The Advocaat-Boekhouder attests
the tallies before they are marked final.

## Commitments

1. **Every prize timber, cask and rope is reused.** A condemned prize's gear, once the Prize
   Council has ruled and the sale is done, goes to the chamber's yard before the auction: rope to
   the rope loft, staves to the cooper, sound timber to the hard.
2. **Oakum is picked from old rope, never from new.** New hemp goes to the ropewalk and nowhere
   else.
3. **Sails are patched to the end**, then cut down to a smaller suit, then cut for tarpaulins,
   hatch covers, hammocks and boat covers. The loft's cuttings go to the kiln, not the water.
4. **A hull broken up is a stock of timber, not a heap of waste.** The yard's book records every
   frame, knee, plank and bolt out of her and where it went.
5. **No tippin' without a reason entered.** Nothin' goes to landfill unless the Harbourmaster has
   entered in the yard's book why it could not be reused, burned for heat or recast.
6. **The articles' rule on gear.** "Nothing aboard or ashore that was not in use on a Dutch ship in the 1600s".
   No mark in this order may be met with a process or material the rule forbids.
7. **Gross marks, never bought.** No mark is met by credits, offsets or reuse bought from others.
   A mark is met by what the yards themselves make over.
8. **Bad news first.** A missed milestone is reported plainly, ahead of the good news, with its
   cause and what will be done about it.

## Standards and shipmates

The order follows ESRS E5 and the waste hierarchy it rests on: reuse before burnin', burnin' for
heat before landfill. The marks have **not** been validated by any outside body, and the Gilde
claims no certificate of circularity.

Whose interests were heard: the master carpenters and coopers, who keep the books on which the
marks stand and proposed the oakum, lye and lead actions; the Harbourmasters, who costed the work
at the hard; the hands, who renew the salvage articles by vote at each Kermis parley; and the
chandlers who fit us out, whose offcuts and old stock the yards take back where they can.

## How it is made known

The salvage articles are read aloud at every Kermis parley and at the signing of the articles by
each new hand. This written order is posted at every chamber gate and at every hard, carried in
each master carpenter's chest, and given to every chandler who supplies timber, hemp, canvas,
iron, tar or provisions.

## Actions FY2026 to FY2030

| # | Action | Where | When | Expected result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Salvage before the auction: prize gear sorted to the yard, loft, cooper and hard before any lot is cried | Every chamber | Every year | Reused share of inflow up toward 15.0% by 2030 |
| A2 | Every hull broken up logged frame by frame in the yard's book, with its destination, in one form for timber, staves and rope | Vlissingen, Port Royal, and any hard that breaks a hull | FY2026, then every year | Broken hull timber put back into the fleet up to 90.0% by 2030 |
| A3 | Tar skimmin's re-boiled and used cold as paying stuff, since tar is the material least made over (6.7% in FY2025); shared with POL-E2 and its mark of 24.0 t boiled | All chamber hards | FY2026 to FY2028 | Secondary tar rises, and the reused share excluding provisions with it |
| A4 | Sails patched, turned and cut down in the loft before new canvas is bought; iron fittings recast at the chamber smithy. This is action A9 of [POL-E1](e1-climate.md), counted here as inflow avoided | Sail lofts, smithies | FY2026 to FY2030 | Fewer purchased tonnes of sailcloth and iron per tonne carried |
| A5 | Galley and kiln ash sent to the ropewalk's lye and the oak plantin' at every chamber, not tipped | Every chamber, Vava'u from its first fire | FY2026 | No ash to landfill |
| A6 | Careenin' scrapin's sorted at the hard: tar-bearing scrapin's to the kettle, lead-bearing to the smithy to be recast; only what neither can take is sealed in casks and entered in the yard's book | All hards | FY2027 to FY2029 | Hazardous waste to landfill down from 1.4 t to 1.0 t by 2030, and to nothin' by 2050 |
| A7 | Worn rope and staves traced from hull to loft and cooper in the same form as the timber, so what is reused is counted and what is lost is seen | Rope loft, cooperage | FY2026 to FY2027 | Firmer outflow figures; less rope and stave waste burned |
| A8 | Vava'u's yard books opened in the form of A2 and A7 from its first hull; its baseline for every mark in this order set in its first full year | Chamber of Vava'u | From first full year | Baseline `[UNCHARTED]` until then |

**Resources.** Every action is done with the yards' own hands, offcuts and tools, and is charged
to the careenin' record or the chamber's ledger. None needs capital expenditure or operatin'
expenditure significant against the Gilde's turnover, so no amounts are given by action (ESRS 2
MDR-A, paragraph 69). Action A3 and the 2050 marks ask for a smith's and a cooper's time that the
Harbourmasters will have to find from within the yards' labour.

The figures in the table are the marks we steer for. FY2026 closed on 2026-09-30; its outcome is
`[UNCHARTED]` until the ledgers for it are closed and sworn at Middelburg. E5-T1 to E5-T4 carry
the 2030 marks set by the Raad at the Kermis of 2025, and their FY2030 milestones equal them. The
2050 marks are new in this order. E5-T5 was first set by this written order, so it has no FY2026
milestone; FY2025, its first counted year, is its baseline, and its FY2024 value is not in the
ledgers. All five cover all 6 chambers and the whole fleet; Vava'u's contribution is
`[UNCHARTED]` until its first full year.

**Why two measures of the reused share.** The Gilde buys 1,310 t of provisions of the 2,960.4 t
purchased, 44.2% of the inflow, and they are eaten, not made over. E5-T1 is the honest total and
is dragged down by the food. E5-T2 leaves the provisions aside, so that the yards are judged on
timber, hemp, canvas, iron and tar, which they can make over. Both are reported; neither replaces
the other. If the share of provisions falls as the long haul is victualled ashore (POL-E1, action
A7), E5-T1 will rise without any more being salvaged, and the Council will say so when it reads
the mark.

**How the 2050 marks were reasoned.** Even if every tonne that can be made over were made over,
provisions would hold E5-T1 well below 100%; a mark of 27.0% for the total goes with 45.0% for
E5-T2, and assumes provisions stay near 40% of input. The hull-timber mark of 95.0% leaves room
for rot and teredo-eaten planks that cannot be saved. A landfill share of 0.0% by 2050 is a
mark held with E5-T5: the lead is recast and the tar re-boiled, and nothin' is tipped. No mark
relies on a process that the articles' rule forbids.

**New targets considered and not set.** A mark for sailcloth bought per voyage has no baseline
in canon, since voyages are not counted in the sailcloth ledger; the Council will set one when
the loft's ledger and the voyage book are brought into one form.

## Monitoring and review

- **By the quarter.** Every yard's timber, rope and stave books and its waste entries go into the
  chamber's ledger. The Harbourmaster reads them and the master carpenter signs the timber book.
- **By the Kermis year.** The Advocaat-Boekhouder closes the year at 30 September and the
  Quartermaster-General reads each milestone against it at the Kermis Council, under E5-3.
- **When a milestone is missed.** The Raad van Kapiteins reports the cause first, names the
  action that will recover it, and does not lower the mark.
- **Review.** The order is renewed at every Kermis Council; the marks are reset no later than the
  Kermis of 2030.
