## Purpose

The Water Cask Order is the Gilde's standin' order (policy) on fresh water and on what we take
from the sea. It does two things. It keeps our draw of fresh water from weighing on the
catchments we share with villages, above all at the two island chambers where the water is
finite and seasonal. And it holds the Gilde to taking nothing from the sea itself: no fish,
whale, coral, shell or seabed.

It answers three of the matters that weigh in [the IRO register](../data/iros.csv): the fresh
water we draw under lease at Île Sainte-Marie and from the Ilen at Baltimore (IRO-08); what the
Gilde might take from the sea, held at nothing (IRO-09); and the risk that a drought at the
island chambers closes the waterin' place and with it the Indian Ocean run (IRO-10).

Water is only partly material to the Gilde, and we say so. Elsewhere our draw is small against
the town's own. We set no mark on the total draw, for it follows the hands we carry and the days
they are at sea, neither of which we would cut for a water target.

## Scope

- **Activities.** All water drawn for casks, for the hands ashore and for yard work
  (careenin', cooperage, the ropewalk and the ovens), and every dealing of the Gilde with the sea's
  living or mineral stock.
- **Where.** All 6 chambers of the Gilde from FY2026, the Chamber of Vava'u included, and every
  hull of the fleet, at sea and in port.
- **Value chain.** Own operations. Water drawn by the chandlers who fit us out is not measured by
  the Gilde and is not covered by a mark.
- **Vava'u.** The chamber opened in October 2025, after the reported year. It has no measured
  value, and its water stress is `[UNCHARTED]` until assessed as set out under A4 below. Nothing
  is estimated in its place.
- **Exclusions.** None.

## Accountability

The Quartermaster-General is the highest office answerable for this order, reports on it to De
Heeren Vijf at every sitting, and reads every milestone against the ledgers at the Kermis
Council. The Harbourmaster of each chamber is answerable for the lease, the cisterns, the yard
and the log of the dry season; the purser of each hull for the casks and the ration tally aboard.
Until De Heeren Vijf appoint a Harbourmaster for Vava'u, the Harbourmaster of Ambon answers for
the chamber's water. The Advocaat-Boekhouder attests the tallies before they are marked final.

## Commitments

1. **On casks.** Casks are filled only at a spring, stream or well the chamber holds under a
   written lease or a Harbourmaster's permission, never at a village's own drawin' place. At
   Sainte-Marie this is the fresh-water lease from the village of Ambodifotatra, renewed each
   Kermis year, with the undertakin' that the forest above the intake be left standin'. A new
   chamber draws no cask until its lease or permission is written.
2. **On yard water.** Careenin', cooperage, the ropewalk and the ovens draw from the chamber's
   cistern and rainwater catch first, and never from the stream in the dry season without the
   Harbourmaster's entry in the log.
3. **On the ration.** 4.5 litre a hand a day at sea and 25 litre a hand a day ashore, never cut
   for economy, only by the surgeon. No water mark is met by cutting the ration.
4. **Nothing taken from the sea.** No fish caught for sale, no whale hunted, no coral, reef stone
   or shell taken, no seabed dredged, no aquaculture, no salt pans. Fish taken over the side by a
   hand is eaten at the mess table. Ambergris is gathered only where it is found on a beach.
   Whale oil and fish oil are bought in, never taken; the Gilde does not keep the gear to take
   either.
5. **Gross marks, never bought.** No mark in this order is met by water credits, offsets or
   anything bought in place of the work. No action or mark may break the articles' rule on gear:
   "Nothing aboard or ashore that was not in use on a Dutch ship in the 1600s".
6. **Bad news first.** A missed milestone is reported plainly, ahead of the good news, with its
   cause and what will be done about it.

## Standards and shipmates

The order follows ESRS E3. Its stress rating is the Gilde's own reading, not that of an outside
body: a chamber is scored high stress where its catchment is fed by a season and shared with a
village whose draw has first call under our lease. Sainte-Marie and Ambon are so scored. The
marks have **not** been validated by any outside body, and every figure of water drawn is
**derived** (hands x days x the ration, with yard water at 35 m3 per hull-year), not metered, for
the technology rule gives us nothing to meter with.

Whose interests were heard: the village of Ambodifotatra, whose council reads the lease each
Kermis year; the Harbourmasters of Sainte-Marie and Ambon, who costed the cisterns; the hands,
whose ration is not for sale; and the surgeon, who alone may cut it.

## How it is made known

This written order is posted at every chamber gate and carried in each purser's sea chest. The
lease at Sainte-Marie is read at the village council each October before renewal. A chandler
or watering place is given the commitments on casks before the first cask is filled.

E3-T1 and E3-T2 were set by the Raad van Kapiteins at the Kermis of 2025 and are unchanged; the

**What the marks assume.** The stressed draw in FY2025 was 31.9 % of the whole, all of it at
Sainte-Marie and Ambon. The mark of 3000 m3 for 2030 is met by A1, as rainwater takes the place
of the stream for the yard's work; the 2050 mark asks for the same road carried further. The
Harbourmasters say plainly that a poor rainy season at either island would put a milestone back
a year, and that the ration at sea and ashore is not to be cut to recover it.

**Vava'u.** E3-T1 counts Sainte-Marie and Ambon only, for these are the chambers scored high
stress. Should the assessment under A4 score Vava'u high stress, its draw will be added to the

years, and the Quartermaster-General will be asked how the mark is to be read. E3-T2 likewise
counts Sainte-Marie and Ambon until Vava'u's yard water has a first full year's baseline, which
is `[UNCHARTED]` today. The mark of 3000 m3 does not move without a change in canon.

## Monitoring and review

- **By the quarter.** Each purser enters the ration tally and each Harbourmaster the yard and
  dry-season log into the chamber's ledger. The Harbourmaster reads both.
- **By the Kermis year.** The Advocaat-Boekhouder closes the year at 30 September and the
  Quartermaster-General reads each milestone against it at the Kermis Council. The reading is
  reported in the reckonin' o' the year (sustainability statement) under E3-3.
- **When a milestone is missed.** The Raad van Kapiteins reports the cause first, names the action
  that will recover it, and does not lower the mark. A mark is never recovered by cutting the
  ration or by buying water credits.
- **Review.** The order is renewed at every Kermis Council; the marks are reset no later than the
  Kermis of 2030.
