## Purpose

The Vow of the Old Ways is the Gilde's standin' order (policy) on the warmin' o' the seas
(climate change). It does two things. It holds the means by which we sail, so that no fuel is
burned and no power bought, in any hull or chamber, for as long as the Gilde sails. And it
sets out how we shall shrink the smoke our old means still make: at the tar kilns, in the
galleys, at the lamps and in the livestock pens of the retourschepen.

It answers four of the matters that weigh in [the IRO register](../data/iros.csv): our own
smoke (IRO-01); the smoke of them as fit us out (IRO-02); the storms that warmer seas bring
to Port Royal, Sainte-Marie and Ambon (IRO-03); and the fair wind of clients who ask for
carriage with no fuel in it (IRO-04).

We do not claim, and do not aim for, net zero. A wooden ship that cooks for her hands will
always make some smoke, and we would rather report the residue than buy it away.

## Scope

- **Activities.** All carriage, interdiction under commission, careenin', ship-buildin' and
  victuallin' done by the Gilde or on its account.
- **Where.** All 6 chambers of the Gilde from FY2026, the Chamber of Vava'u included, and all
  25 hulls of the fleet, at sea and in port.
- **Value chain.** Own operations (Scope 1 and Scope 2) and windward o' us (Scope 3: the oak,
  hemp, canvas, iron and provisions the chandlers make on our account). Leeward o' us there is
  nothin' to burn: a voyage is consumed as it is made.
- **Exclusions.** None. No hull, chamber or season is exempt, and no exemption has been granted
  since the written order was adopted.

## Accountability

The Quartermaster-General keeps the Vow as she keeps the common chest. She is the highest
office answerable for this order, reports on it to De Heeren Vijf at every sitting, and reads
every milestone against the ledgers at the Kermis Council. The Harbourmaster of each chamber is
answerable for the kilns and the hard; the master of each hull for her galley, lamps, powder
and livestock. The Advocaat-Boekhouder attests the tallies before they are marked final.

## Commitments

1. **No fuel, no bought power.** The articles' rule on gear:
   "Nothing aboard or ashore that was not in use on a Dutch ship in the 1600s". Forbidden gear goes over the side, and the hand
   who brought it aboard forfeits a share.
2. **The standin' order on galley fuel.** No green wood in a galley where seasoned wood or the
   yard's own charcoal can be had; hearths covered at sea and in port; the cook's fire drawn at
   the end of the second dog watch unless the surgeon asks otherwise.
3. **The standin' order on tar.** Tar and pitch boiled under a hood, never in the open; kettles
   sized to the work in hand; every kettle fired entered in the careenin' record with the weight
   boiled. A kiln lit at a new chamber is hooded before its first fire.
4. **Gross marks, never bought.** No mark in this order is met by carbon credits, offsets or
   removals bought from others. Trees the Gilde plants are not claimed against any mark.
5. **Bad news first.** A missed milestone is reported plainly, ahead of the good news, with its
   cause and what will be done about it.

## Standards and shipmates

The boundary of Scope 1, 2 and 3 follows ESRS E1, which rests on the Greenhouse Gas Protocol
Corporate Standard. The marks have **not** been validated by the Science Based Targets
initiative or any other outside body. The fall of 41% in fossil Scope 1 from FY2024 to 2030 is
steeper than the 4.2% a year of the cross-sector 1.5 °C pathway, but we have not had that
comparison checked and do not claim alignment on the strength of it.

Whose interests were heard: the hands, who renew the Vow by vote at each Kermis parley and
proposed the beeswax and pressed-oil actions; the Harbourmasters, who costed the kiln work; the
town of Vlissingen, whose complaints about tar smoke shaped the kiln actions; and the clients
of the Letters of Marque Division who asked for carriage with no fuel in it.

## How it is made known

The Vow is read aloud at every Kermis parley and at the signing of the articles by each new
hand. This written order is posted at every chamber gate, carried in each master's sea chest,
and given to every chandler who supplies oak, hemp, canvas, iron or provisions.

## Actions FY2026 to FY2030

| # | Action | Where | When | Expected result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Smaller kettles and no second boil. Kettles sized to the plank, not the hull; tar that has cooled is used cold as paying stuff rather than boiled again | All chamber hards | FY2026, fully by FY2027 | Tar boiled down to 24.0 t a year by 2030, shared with POL-E2 |
| A2 | Kiln hoods at Baltimore and Sainte-Marie through their first full year; a hood over any kiln lit at Vava'u before its first fire | Baltimore, Sainte-Marie, Vava'u | FY2026 | Every kiln of the Gilde hooded |
| A3 | Charcoal in every galley that can carry it, beyond the retourschepen, jachten and sloops already moved; charcoal pits dug at Baltimore and Sainte-Marie from coppice offcuts | Fluyts and galjoots; Baltimore, Sainte-Marie | Pits FY2027, all galleys FY2028 | Less firewood and biogenic smoke; covered hearths fleet-wide |
| A4 | Pressed oil from fish and rapeseed in every lamp but the sailmakers' close-work lamps; pressed at Sainte-Marie, Vlissingen and Ambon | All hulls and chambers | FY2026 to FY2028 | Whale oil limited to the sail lofts |
| A5 | A sworn smoke-per-measure (emission factor) for pressed oil, so that it is no longer booked at the whale-oil factor; the change and its effect restated against FY2024 | Middelburg, by the Advocaat-Boekhouder | FY2027 | Lamp-oil smoke reported at its true weight |
| A6 | Beeswax in place of tallow: cabins and the great cabin lanterns of the retourschepen first, then every hull | Retourschepen, then the fleet | FY2027 to FY2029 | Tallow line nearly gone by 2030, gone by 2050 |
| A7 | Fewer beasts on the hoof. The long haul victualled with fresh meat ashore at Sainte-Marie, Ambon and Vava'u, so each retourschip sails with half the sheep and hens | The 4 retourschepen | FY2026 to FY2030 | Livestock carried down from 640 head to 320 by 2030 |
| A8 | Flags before guns: signals made by flag and lantern wherever a gun signal is not needed for safety; salutes fired only where custom of the port requires | All hulls | FY2026 | Black powder fired down by more than a third |
| A9 | Mend before you buy: sails patched and turned before new canvas is bought, iron fittings recast at the chamber, more cargo on the homeward legs | Sail lofts, smithies, the voyage book | FY2026 to FY2030 | Scope 3 per million tonne-nmi down to 60 by 2030 |
| A10 | Weather-wise sailin': departures held to each route's season window, hulls home before the cyclone season on PAC-1, the mangrove behind the Port Royal warehouses kept standin' | All routes; Port Royal | Every year | Fewer hulls caught at sea by storms (adaptation, IRO-03) |

**Resources.** Every action is done with the yards' own hands, timber offcuts and tools, and is
charged to the careenin' record or the chamber's ledger. None needs capital expenditure or
operating expenditure that is significant against the Gilde's turnover, so no amounts are
given by action (ESRS 2 MDR-A, paragraph 69). Action A7 will raise the salt provisions bought
and therefore Scope 3; that cost is inside the Scope 3 mark and will be shown when it comes.

| Tallow candles | 6.4 | 2.2 | 0 | A6 |

The FY2025 lines are rounded as in E1-6 and sum to 168.1; the unrounded tally is 168.0.

Tar is the line that decides the mark. The 2030 budget of 76.8 tCO2e is 24.0 t of tar boiled,
the same mark POL-E2 holds for the kilns; the 2050 budget asks for 10.0 t. Livestock is the
second: halving the 640 head on the hoof depends on fresh meat being had ashore at the far
chambers, and the Harbourmasters say plainly that a bad season at Sainte-Marie would put that
back a year.

Climate adaptation (IRO-03) has actions (A10) but **no measurable target yet**. The Council will
set one at the Kermis of 2027, once a full year's voyage book shows how often a hull was held in
port or caught at sea by weather.

## Monitoring and review

- **By the quarter.** Every hull's galley, lamp and powder tally goes into the chamber's ledger;
  every kettle fired goes into the careenin' record. The Harbourmaster reads both.
- **By the Kermis year.** The Advocaat-Boekhouder closes the year at 30 September and the
  Quartermaster-General reads each milestone against it at the Kermis Council. The reading is
  reported in the reckonin' o' the year (sustainability statement) under E1-4.
- **When a milestone is missed.** The Raad van Kapiteins reports the cause first, names the
  action that will recover it, and does not lower the mark. If the 2030 mark for Scope 1 is
  missed, the Council revisits an internal carbon price, which it declined at the Kermis of 2025.
- **Review.** The order is renewed at every Kermis Council; the marks are reset no later than the
  Kermis of 2030.
