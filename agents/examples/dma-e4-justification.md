# Example — the reckonin' o' what matters, E4

**Request.** The platform's DMA module asks: why be E4 material?

**Answer as submitted**

```text
Topic: E4 Biodiversity and ecosystems — the livin' coasts an' forests — Outcome: material (impact)

Why: In the reckonin' o' what matters (double materiality assessment) the Gilde scored 30
IROs on bounded criteria; a negative impact weighs when its severity, the mean o' scale,
scope an' irremediability, multiplied by its likelihood reaches the threshold o' 9 on a
scale o' 25. Four impacts under E4 cross it, an' none o' them be a fair wind. IRO-11, the
oak an' pine felled for hulls, masts an' spars windward o' us, be an actual impact: severity
3.7 at likelihood 5, score 18.5. IRO-13, the rats, weed an' ballast sand our wooden hulls
carry between 5 chambers of operation across three oceans, scores 16.0 at severity 4.0 an'
likelihood 4; 'tis the gravest, for what a rat does to an island cannot be undone. IRO-14,
the exotic materials bought from island forests, scores 15.0. IRO-12, careenin' an' ballast
work on the cays at Port Royal an' the sand at Sainte-Marie beside reef an' mangrove, be
the borderline matter at 9.9, an' the Council chose to let it weigh rather than argue it
down. No financial risk under E4 crossed the financial threshold, so the topic be material
on impact alone.

IROs: IRO-11 (negative impact, 18.5), IRO-13 (negative impact, 16.0), IRO-14 (negative
impact, 15.0), IRO-12 (negative impact, 9.9)
Sources: csrd/double-materiality.md §The method, §The outcome; data/iros.csv; canon:
dma.impact_threshold, dma.iro_count; csrd/e2-e5-environment.md §E4
```

**Why it passes.** Quotes the IRO rows an' scores rather than re-scorin'; names the
threshold from canon; states the borderline case openly; formal register with the
glossary's DMA vocabulary an' the ESRS code untouched.
