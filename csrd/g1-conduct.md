# G1 Business Conduct — conduct under the articles

Bad news first, as the articles require. In FY2025 one master of this company gave a gift
to a port official at Port Royal above the ceiling the articles set, and did not report it.
The gift ledger found it, not the master; he repaid EUR 660 into the common chest and was
fined one share. One confirmed incident in the year, against none in FY2024, and it stands
before anything we did well.

This is the account of the Neederlandsch Schuijnvaerders Gilde N.V. of its conduct under
the articles (ESRS G1 business conduct) for FY2025, the reportin' year 102024-092025,
against FY2024, the base year 102023-092024. It rests on four papers: the commissie
register at Middelburg, the Prize Council's sentence book, the gift ledger of each of the
5 chambers of operation, and the Advocaat-Boekhouder's payment book. Every EUR figure is a
Kermis year total, closed on 30 September and sworn.

## G1 SBM-3 an' IRO-1

Four conduct matters weigh on this company, and one weighs more than the rest.

**The interdiction service is the central conduct question.** The Letters of Marque
Division stops and examines other people's ships on a client's commission, and everythin'
that can go wrong in our conduct can go wrong there: a commission taken from a client who
should not have one, a mark stopped that should have been let by, cargo broken into before
a court has spoken, a ransom secured by holdin' a person rather than a paper. The impact
falls outward, on the crews and owners of the ships we stop, and it is actual, not
potential, because we stopped ships this year. An interdiction wrongly made would cost us
restitution, our licence and our standin' with clients. Material on both sides.

**Corruption at ports.** We call at harbours where a gift to the harbourmaster is custom
and not crime, and the line between custom and bribe is thin, local and unwritten. A
harbour where our masters buy their berth is a worse harbour for every other ship; back on
us the risk is a fine, a lost berth and a client who will not be seen with us. Material,
and where our only FY2025 incident arose.

**The honesty of the auction.** A prize ruled good prize is sold at open auction, and
company, client, chamber and crew all take a share of what it fetches, so every party has a
reason to want the hammer to fall in a particular place. A rigged auction robs the owner we
took the goods from and the crew we owe a share to in one stroke. Material.

**Supplier payment.** The rope-walks, yards and chandlers that fit us out are small houses
with small chests, and a late payment from us is their lean month. Material as an impact,
small as a risk to us.

**How they are assessed.** The Quartermaster-General assesses all four each Kermis year:
she counts the counted, holds the parley book, reads every entry in the gift ledger, and
reports to the Raad van Kapiteins at every sittin' and to De Heeren Vijf at every quarter.
The Prize Council at Middelburg assesses the interdiction question file by file — four
ordinary sittings, and one within forty days of any seizure — and every refusal and
restitution passes from its sentence book into this file.


## G1-1 Corporate culture an' business conduct policies

**The articles are the culture.** We keep no separate code of conduct: we have had articles
of agreement since 1621 that every hand signs before a first watch is stood. They are
amended only by a vote of all hands, one hand one vote, and every amendment is dated in the
margin of the Vlissingen copy and never struck out, so that what the company has repented
of can still be read.

**Compaen is the cautionary tale.** Claes Compaen sailed with a lawful commission, dropped
it in 1623, took hundreds of ships on his own account and ended petitionin' for a pardon.
His story is read aloud at every muster beside the articles, and ends the same way each
time: *the licence is the whole of the difference between this company and a thief.* It is
why an unregistered commission is mutiny, not paperwork.

The standin' orders (policies) under the articles are these.

| Standing order | What it requires | Where written |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client due diligence for a commissie | No commission served until registered at Middelburg, a bond posted, all 8 refusal conditions initialled | Articles 17, 18 |
| Anti-corruption | Port dues paid openly; customary gifts capped per port call and entered by name in the gift ledger; no gift to win or keep a commission, at any price | Article 22 |
| Whistleblowin' — the parley | Any hand, and any person ashore in a port we use, may call the Quartermaster; the call is entered in the parley book with its outcome | Article 16 |
| Protection of the caller | No hand is turned out of a ship, docked or shifted for callin' the parley; a breach is itself a dismissal matter | Article 16 |
| Animal welfare | Livestock carried for fresh meat is pastured on deck, watered daily, never above what the deck will hold, and slaughtered by the cook out of sight of the rest | Article 22 |
| Supplier relations | Payment in EUR within thirty days, or in shares of the voyage where the supplier elects; no set-off against a disputed line | Article 7 |

**The Quartermaster-General's independence, and her veto.** The officer who receives a call
is elected by all hands, one hand one vote, not appointed by the board she reports on, and
none who has held a command in the three years before may stand. No distribution leaves the
common chest over her veto, which only the Raad van Kapiteins and De Heeren Vijf votin' the
same way in the same quarter may override, and the override goes into the reckonin'. She is
heard by the Muster alone and may investigate any chamber, hull or ledger without leave.
One string ties an officer's purse to this: a fifth of every officer's share is held in the
common chest until she has counted the year's hurts and tally and De Heeren Vijf have
approved the reckonin'. There is no bonus elsewhere.

**Calls received.** The parley book records **7 calls on a conduct matter** in FY2025: five
on a gift or a port payment, one on a supplier's unpaid bill, one on a boardin'. All seven
were heard and closed within the year, **no caller was put out of a ship, docked or
shifted**, and no complaint of retaliation was made.

**Learnin' the ropes.** Every hand hears the articles read at signin' and at the close of
each Kermis year: 2,294 souls all told, 100%, at sea and ashore. The 25 masters and the
5 Harbourmasters also take the gift-and-port-dues session each October — 30 persons, 100%
of those who hold a purse in a foreign port — and the Division's officers read the refusal
conditions aloud, one by one, before the season's first commission.

## The commissie due diligence

This is the procedure an auditor may test, file by file, against the register: the 5 steps
of the Letters of Marque Division expanded into the acts actually performed, each leavin' a
paper.

1. **Application and registration.** A client applies at the Middelburg chamber; no other
   chamber may take an application and no officer at sea may take one. The
   Advocaat-Boekhouder's registrar — a clerk holdin' no command and takin' no share of any
   prize — enters the letter of marque under a running number with its date of receipt.
2. **What the client must show.** Before a number is issued the registrar copies into the
   register: (a) the client's **identity** and legal form, with the deed naming who may
   sign; (b) the **cause**, with the papers evidencing it; (c) the **vessels** named as
   marks and the **waters** they may be stopped in; (d) the **term**, with a closing date;
   (e) a sworn declaration that the client **is not at war with the Netherlands**; (f) a
   sworn declaration that the target is **not under truce and flies no flag of the
   Netherlands, the EU, or an ally of either**.
3. **The bond.** The client posts a bond at Middelburg of **20% of the expected prize
   value**, and not less than **EUR 250,000**, before any hull sails. The expected value is
   the registrar's estimate, not the client's. It answers for restitution and the costs of
   a failed seizure, and is returned only on the sentence.
4. **The 8 refusal conditions, checked and initialled one by one.** The registrar works
   down the printed list and sets initials and a date against each: (1) no seizure of
   vessels of the Netherlands, the EU or the client's declared allies; (2) no commission
   from a client at war with the Netherlands, none against a party under truce; (3) no
   breakin' bulk or sale before adjudication; (4) no seizure without inspection of papers,
   clean papers meanin' release with costs to the client; (5) no work outside the waters or
   term named; (6) no pressin' of captured crew; (7) no hostage-takin' to secure a ransom
   bill; (8) no attack under a flag of truce or in a neutral harbour. **A line without
   initials voids the registration.**
5. **Issue and sailin' orders.** Only then is the number issued and the waters and term
   copied into the assigned hull's sailin' orders.
6. **The stop and the interrogatory aboard.** The mark is stopped under superior sail and
   numbers, weapons shown and not used. Her papers and crew are examined aboard under a
   **written interrogatory** — the same questions in the same order in every ocean — signed
   by the examinin' officer and the master examined, who keeps a copy. Clean papers mean
   release on the spot, costs to the client.
7. **Seals, and no bulk broken.** Seized cargo is sealed where it lies under the chamber's
   device in wax, a tally number against each seal, and carried to the nearest chamber: no
   hatch opened, no sample drawn, nothin' sold. Seals are broken at the chamber, in
   daylight, before the Advocaat-Boekhouder's clerk.
8. **The Prize Council sentence.** It sits within forty days of the cargo bein' landed under
   seal — one bewindhebber not of the chamber that brought the prize in, one captain not of
   the ship that took her, and the Advocaat-Boekhouder or her deputy — and rules **good
   prize** or orders **restitution in full**, the costs fallin' on the client. The
   Quartermaster-General sits of right.
9. **Auction under public notice.** A prize ruled good prize is sold at open auction, the
   notice posted at the chamber and the port fourteen days before, the sale under the bell
   with the Advocaat-Boekhouder's clerk recordin' every bid and bidder. No officer, hand or
   relation may bid. Proceeds are divided net of costs on the published table: **40** to
   the client, 30 to the company, 10 to the chamber fee, 20 to the crew.
10. **The ransom bill as a bond only.** In place of sale a vessel may be released against a
    written ransom bill. It secures a debt on paper, never a person, and no hostage is
    taken to support it.
11. **The register entries kept.** Each commission carries a file at Middelburg:
    application, client's papers, initialled refusal list, bond receipt, sailin' orders,
    interrogatory, seal tally, sentence, and the auction sheet or ransom bill.

**FY2025, plainly.** **12 commissions were applied for**; **3 were refused at registration**
and never issued: one on **condition 1**, the named target flyin' the flag of an EU Member
State; one on **condition 2**, the client's own declaration showin' the party complained of
to be under truce; one on **condition 5**, the waters asked for lyin' outside anythin' that
could be worked within the term named.

The remainin' **9 commissions were served**: **5 ended in good prize**, 4 sold at auction
and 1 released against a ransom bill; **2 ended in restitution** ordered by the Prize
Council, in full, at the client's cost; **2 ended in release on the spot**, the papers bein'
clean, again at the client's cost. No hand of this company and no hand of any vessel stopped
was killed or gravely hurt.

## G1-2 Management o' relationships with suppliers

How the Gilde chooses, treats an' pays the houses that fit it out is written out in [POL-G1-2 The Thirty Days Order](../policies/g1-supplier-relations.md), with its marks to 2050.

The chandlers, rope-walks and yards that fit us out numbered **96** in FY2025 across the
5 chambers of operation: Baltic yards for oak, hemp and tar; the rope-walks and sail lofts
at Vlissingen and Ambon; victuallers and coopers at the other three chambers. Most are
small houses; several are one family.

A bill is paid in EUR within **thirty days** of its date, or in **shares of the voyage where
the supplier elects it** — a supplier takin' shares stands beside the crew in the divide and
carries the same risk of a lean season. **11 suppliers elected shares in FY2025.** No
set-off is made against a disputed line.

We paid in **24 days** on average against the thirty-day term, with **96.4%** of invoices
within terms. **2 late-payment disputes** were raised against us, both by small yards over
a bill mislaid between a chamber and Middelburg, both settled in full. **No legal
proceedings for late payment** were brought against us, or by us. A Harbourmaster with an
interest in a supplier declares it and does not sign that supplier's bills.


## G1-3 Prevention an' detection o' corruption an' bribery

Port dues, wharfage, anchorage, pilotage and lastgeld are charges, not gifts: paid against
the port's receipt at its published rate. Beside them sit the customary gifts — a cask of
wine, a barrel of salt beef, a hat to the pilot. We cap them and write them down.

**The ceiling.** The articles set **EUR 250 per port call**, countin' every gift to every
official of that port together, and it may not be split across two calls, two hulls or two
names to get round it. No sum permits a gift to win, keep or speed a commission, a berth
ahead of another ship, or a release: that is not a gift. Such a demand is refused, the ship
waits, and the master reports it the same day. **4 such demands were refused and reported
in FY2025**, and none was paid.

**How each is entered.** Every gift goes into the **gift ledger** of the chamber the hull is
enrolled at, on the day it is given, with the date, the port, the hull, the master who gave
it, the thing given, its value in EUR, and **the name of the harbourmaster or port official
who received it**. No entry says "customary" or "sundry". In FY2025 the five ledgers carried
**187 entries** totallin' **EUR 21,400**.

**Who reviews it.** The chamber's Harbourmaster certifies her ledger monthly and may certify
nothin' she has not seen; the Advocaat-Boekhouder consolidates the five half-yearly and
attests the total. The **Quartermaster-General reads every entry**, not a sample, at the
Kermis close, settin' it against the port calls in each hull's log; an entry with no port
call, or a port call with no entry, is queried and answered in writin'. She may open any
chest, ledger or log without leave, take evidence under the parley, and put a finding to
both tiers at the same sittin'.

**Trainin'.** The October session works the ceiling, the ledger form and the previous
year's refusals through case by case with the 25 masters and the 5 Harbourmasters — 30
persons, 100% — because they are the hands that hold the purse in a foreign port.

## G1-4 Incidents o' corruption or bribery

One confirmed incident in FY2025, none in FY2024, set out first and in full.

At Port Royal, in the second quarter of the Kermis year, the master of the sloop *Squall*
(NSG-20) gave a cask of wine and a barrel of salt beef to the island port authority's
wharfinger, together worth **EUR 660**, against the ceiling of EUR 250. He entered the gift
under the official's name — which is why we know of it — but did not report the excess; no
berth, release or advantage was asked for or given. The Quartermaster-General's Kermis
review found it. He repaid the whole **EUR 660**, was heard as Article 15 requires, and was
**fined one share, EUR 3,850**. He was not dismissed; the ledger review moved to every
half-year at all 5 chambers; and the Advocaat-Boekhouder wrote to the officer who received
the gift, naming the ceiling.

## G1-5 Political influence an' lobbying

The rule of open petition is written out in [POL-G1-5 The Open Petition Order](../policies/g1-political-engagement.md), with its marks to 2050.

This company pays **no political money**: no contribution, in EUR or in kind, to any party,
candidate, campaign, committee or foundation in FY2025 or FY2024, **EUR 0** in both years.
There is no political arm, no subscription any part of which is political, and no officer
holds public office. It petitions instead, in its own name, in the open. **3 petitions in
FY2025**:

| Petition | Lodged with | Asked for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The sail-only berth | the port authority at Vlissingen | a berth reachable without a tow, kept for vessels under sail alone |
| The careenin' hard | the port authority at Baltimore | leave to careen in February and March, outside the grey seals' whelpin' |
| Carriage under sail | the Zeeland provincial estates | that carriage under sail alone be recognised in the province's carriage accountin' |

All three stand on the public record of the body they were lodged with, signed by the
Captain-General and countersigned by the Quartermaster-General. **No registered lobbying
was carried on in FY2025**: we are in no transparency register and instruct no
representative. If that changes, the entry will be named here first.

## Method an' evidence

- **The commissie register** at Middelburg: a runnin' number per application, the client's
  papers, the initialled list of the 8 refusal conditions, the bond receipt and the sailin'
  orders. Evidence for the due diligence section, the 3 refusals included.
- **The Prize Council's sentence book**: every sentence, restitution, refusal and dissent,
  the interrogatory and seal tally behind it. Evidence for the 9 commissions served.
- **The gift ledger**, one per chamber, certified monthly, consolidated half-yearly, read
  entry by entry by the Quartermaster-General. Evidence for G1-3 and for the FY2025
  incident, which it is also the means that found.
- **The Advocaat-Boekhouder's payment book** at Vlissingen: every bill, its date, due date
  and payment date, per chamber and supplier. Evidence for G1-2 and G1-6, over every
  invoice and not a sample.

Two judgements are worth flaggin'. Days to pay counts from the date of the bill, not the
date it reached a chamber: the harder measure, and the one that shows our own delay. And
the gift ledger records what a chamber paid for a thing, not what it might fetch in the
port where it was given; the Quartermaster-General may re-value an entry upward, and did
so twice.
