# The Reckonin' o' What Matters — the double materiality assessment

Bad news first. Of the 30 matters we weighed, 21 be negative impacts an' only 3 be
positive ones, an' the two heaviest negatives be not the weather nor the smoke: they be the
hurts our own hands take in our service, an' the forests windward o' us that our hulls eat,
both scored at 18.5 on a scale o' 25.
Two more be uncomfortable an' we name 'em plainly: the share system does not guarantee a
livin' wage in every port we touch, an' the crews o' the vessels stopped by our
Letters of Marque Division have their voyage broken whether the seizure stands or falls.

This file be the Neederlandsch Schuijnvaerders Gilde N.V.'s reckonin' o' what matters
(double materiality assessment, DMA) for FY2025, the reportin' year 102024-092025, against
the base year 102023-092024. It sets out who did the weighin', by what criteria, against
what thresholds, an' what came out. The register o' every matter weighed be
[the IRO register](../data/iros.csv); the method below be what that register's numbers mean.

## The method

**Who owns it.** The Quartermaster-General owns the reckonin' o' what matters, as she owns
the common chest an' the parley book, an' she be the DMA owner on the Salacia platform. She
put the method to the Raad van Kapiteins in April 2025 an' the outcome to De Heeren Vijf
sittin' as the Muster in October 2025, an' both be minuted. The Advocaat-Boekhouder attested
every figure that entered a score from a ledger.

**Who was heard.** Three hearings fed the scorin':

1. **The Kermis parley**, held at each o' the 5 chambers of operation in the closin' weeks
   o' the year, open to all hands at sea an' ashore, one hand one vote on what belonged on
   the list. 41 matters were put up; 30 survived the mergin' o' duplicates.
2. **The Harbourmasters' port hearings**, one at each chamber, where the folk o' the ports
   — village councils, fishers, growers, the town o' Vlissingen, the city o' Ambon — were
   asked what a chamber costs 'em an' what it gives 'em. The tar smoke, the powder days,
   the moorin's an' the water lease all come from those tables.
3. **The clients an' the counting house.** The spice houses, the collectors an' the
   Division's commission clients were asked in writin' what they need o' us; the
   Advocaat-Boekhouder's ledgers supplied what each matter costs or earns.

**How a matter be scored.** Every matter be scored on bounded criteria, each o' 'em an
integer from 1 to 5, an' the bounds be the whole o' the judgement: a 3 be a 3 everywhere.

| What be scored | Criteria | Bounds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A negative impact (what we do to the world) | scale, scope, irremediability | 1 to 5 each |
| A positive impact | scale, scope | 1 to 5 each |
| Any impact | likelihood; an actual impact takes 5 | 1 to 5 |
| A risk or an opportunity (what the world does to our purse) | financial magnitude, financial likelihood | 1 to 5 each |

**The sums.** Severity be the mean o' scale, scope an' irremediability, to one decimal — for
a positive impact, the mean o' scale an' scope, for ye cannot fail to remedy a good turn.
The impact score be severity times likelihood, on a scale o' 25. The financial score be
magnitude times likelihood, on the same scale o' 25.

**The thresholds.** An impact weighs (be material) when its impact score reaches **9** on
the 25 scale, or when it be an actual impact o' severity 4 or more. A matter weighs
financially when its financial score reaches **9**. A matter that crosses either line
enters the reckonin'; a topic weighs when any matter under it crosses either line. The two
lines be set at the same height on purpose: the Gilde does not hold its purse dearer than
its wake. The Raad van Kapiteins approved both thresholds in April 2025 an' they be held
fixed for the next three reckonin's so that a year may be compared with a year.

## Materiality topics

The Gilde keeps its own topic register in its own words, hung on the Standards' topics one
to one, so that a hand at the parley an' a landsman in Brussels weigh the same thing.

| Code | The Gilde's term | ESRS topic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | the warmin' o' the seas | E1 Climate change |
| E2 | what fouls the water an' the air | E2 Pollution |
| E3 | fresh water an' the sea's bounty | E3 Water and marine resources |
| E4 | the livin' coasts an' forests | E4 Biodiversity and ecosystems |
| E5 | makin' do an' makin' over | E5 Resource use and circular economy |
| S1 | our hands | S1 Own workforce |
| S2 | the hands that fit us out | S2 Workers in the value chain |
| S3 | the folk o' the ports | S3 Affected communities |
| S4 | them as take delivery | S4 Consumers and end-users |
| G1 | conduct under the articles | G1 Business conduct |

Beneath each topic sit the Standards' own sub-topics, named in the `esrs_subtopic` column
o' the register: mitigation an' adaptation under E1; air an' water under E2; withdrawals
an' marine resources under E3; land-use, sea-use, invasive species an' the state o' species
under E4; inflows, outflows an' waste under E5; an' so on through the social topics an'
conduct. No entity-specific topic beyond the Standards' list was found to weigh: the
Division's work sits under S2, S4 an' G1, an' the Vow of the Old Ways be a policy, not a
topic.

## The IROs

Thirty matters, one line each, grouped as the register groups 'em. Scores an' thresholds
be in [the register](../data/iros.csv).

**E1, the warmin' o' the seas.** Our own smoke from galley fires, tar kilns, powder an'
lamp oil, 168.0 tCO2e of fossil Scope 1 (IRO-01). The smoke o' them as fit us out, in
Scope 3 (IRO-02). The weather itself: longer storm seasons at three chambers, an' a fleet
under sail cannot outrun what it did not see comin' (IRO-03). An' the fair wind: carriage
with no fuel in it, asked for by clients countin' their own chains (IRO-04).

**E2, what fouls the water an' the air.** Tar an' pitch smoke from the kilns, which the
town o' Vlissingen has complained o' for three centuries (IRO-05). Lead sheathin'
scrapin's, old tar an' spent shot at the careenin' beaches an' powder grounds (IRO-06).
Bilge an' ballast let go in harbour water where a standin' order be not kept (IRO-07).

**E3, fresh water an' the sea's bounty.** Fresh water drawn under lease at Île Sainte-Marie
an' from the Ilen at Baltimore, in catchments shared with a village (IRO-08). What we take
from the sea itself, which be found ambergris an' pressed fish oil an' nothin' else
(IRO-09). An' the risk that drought closes the waterin' place an' with it the Indian Ocean
run (IRO-10).

**E4, the livin' coasts an' forests.** Oak an' pine felled for hulls, masts an' spars, in
forests that be not ours (IRO-11). Careenin' an' ballast work beside reef, mangrove an'
whale water (IRO-12). Rats, weed an' ballast sand carried between 5 chambers of operation
across three oceans (IRO-13). Exotic materials — sandalwood, pearl shell, camphor, gum
copal, logwood — taken from island forests an' reefs (IRO-14).

**E5, makin' do an' makin' over.** A wooden ship be near wholly made over, timber to
timber, rope to oakum, canvas to patch, iron to iron (IRO-15). An' salvage sold on under
the bell at Port Royal an' at the Vlissingen yard (IRO-16).

**S1, our hands.** Hurts taken in the Gilde's service, an' sickness far from a surgeon
(IRO-17). The share system an' the livin'-wage risk under it (IRO-18). Pressin', abolished
in 1697 an' reported as the remediated wrong it be (IRO-19). An' the good the articles do:
one hand one vote, the parley, the Matanzas schedule, the hardship chest an' the yards'
apprenticeships (IRO-20).

**S2, the hands that fit us out.** Conditions at the rope-walks, sawmills, sail lofts an'
victuallers windward o' us (IRO-21). The crews o' seized vessels, whose voyage be broken
whether the Prize Council rules good prize or restitution (IRO-22). Dock labour hired by
the day at the ports o' call, off the muster roll (IRO-23).

**S3, the folk o' the ports.** What a chamber costs its port — smoke, powder days,
moorin's, a ship's company ashore (IRO-24) — an' what a chamber gives it in wages, leases,
schools an' trade (IRO-25).

**S4, them as take delivery.** Provenance: a wrongly papered bale reachin' a spice house
under our own warrant (IRO-26). An' harm to the person o' an end-user, which we looked for
an' did not find, for we deliver to houses an' agents an' never to persons (IRO-27).

**G1, conduct under the articles.** A seizure made on an ill-founded commission, with a
third party's trade broken an' restitution owed (IRO-28). Gifts to harbourmasters, port
dues an' pilotage paid in coin where we be strangers (IRO-29). An' the bond posted at
Middelburg, with the costs o' a failed seizure where a client cannot meet 'em (IRO-30).

## The outcome

ESRS 2 applies always an' be not subject to this reckonin'. Of the ten topic Standards,
eight weigh in full, two weigh in part, an' none be set aside.

| ESRS topic | Material | Why, in one line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ESRS 2 General disclosures | yes, always | Applies to every undertakin' reportin' under the Standards, whatever this reckonin' found |
| E1 Climate change | yes, impact an' financial | Our own smoke an' our chandlers' smoke on the impact side; storm seasons at three chambers on the purse side |
| E2 Pollution | yes, impact | Tar an' pitch smoke, lead an' spent shot at the careenin' beaches, an' bilge an' ballast in harbour water |
| E3 Water and marine resources | partial | Water withdrawal at the island chambers weighs an' carries a drought risk; marine resources do not, for we take nothing from the sea but wind, found ambergris an' pressed fish oil |
| E4 Biodiversity and ecosystems | yes, impact | Timber for hulls, careenin' beside reef an' mangrove, invasive species in ballast an' bilge, an' exotic materials out o' island forests |
| E5 Resource use and circular economy | yes, impact an' financial | A wooden ship be a circular economy that predates the word, an' salvage be a trade in its own right |
| S1 Own workforce | yes, impact an' financial | Hurts an' hazardous work, the livin'-wage risk under the share system, an' the protections the articles give |
| S2 Workers in the value chain | yes, impact | The hands at the rope-walks an' yards, the dock labour off the roll, an' the crews o' the vessels we stop |
| S3 Affected communities | yes, impact | Five ports an' two island communities bear our smoke, our powder days an' our thirst, an' hold our wages an' leases |
| S4 Consumers and end-users | partial | Provenance o' what we deliver weighs; the personal safety o' an end-user does not, for we deliver to houses an' agents, never to persons |
| G1 Business conduct | yes, impact an' financial | Due diligence on every commission, gifts in the ports, an' the bond an' the costs o' a failed seizure |

**On the two partials.** E3 an' S4 be reported under their material sub-topics only, an'
the immaterial sub-topics be stated as immaterial with the reason, which be the disclosure
the Standards ask for. We say plainly that IRO-09 an' IRO-27 fell below the line on the
numbers an' not on the argument, an' both will be weighed again next Kermis.

**Borderline matters.** Three crossed at exactly 9: bilge an' ballast discharge (IRO-07),
the conditions o' the hands that fit us out (IRO-21) an' a seizure on an ill-founded
commission (IRO-28), which also weighs financially at 12. A fourth, careenin' beside reef
an' mangrove (IRO-12), crossed at 9.9. All four be reported as material an' all four be the
first the Quartermaster-General will re-score when the next year's tallies be in. The one
matter scored high an' still found immaterial be pressin' (IRO-19), severity 4.0 an'
likelihood 1: abolished in 1697, absent from both reported years, an' disclosed under S1
regardless, because a remediated wrong that goes unmentioned be a wrong twice.

## Stakeholder engagement

The parley be the Gilde's grievance procedure an' the root o' this reckonin'. Any hand, an'
any person ashore in any port we use, may call it; it be heard by the chamber first an' by
the Quartermaster-General on appeal; it be entered in the parley book with its outcome; an'
no hand may be turned out o' a ship, docked or shifted for callin' it. Of the 2,294 souls
all told under the articles, every one had a voice at the Kermis parley an' a vote on what
went up the list.

Beyond the hands, four groups were heard an' their words be traceable to a row in the
register. The folk o' the ports, through the Harbourmasters' hearings at all 5 chambers of
operation. The clients — spice houses, collectors an' the 9 commission clients the Letters
of Marque Division served in the year — in writin'. The port authorities an' village
councils, through the leases an' undertakin's each chamber holds. An' the crews o' the
vessels stopped in the year, through the interrogatories taken aboard an' the Prize
Council's files, which be the only voice those crews have in our papers an' the reason
IRO-22 stands where it does.

The outcome be laid before the Raad van Kapiteins in April 2025, approved by De Heeren
Vijf sittin' as the Muster in October 2025, an' read back at the Kermis so the hands who
raised the matters could hear where they landed.
