# E2 to E5 Environment — what fouls the water, the fresh water, the livin' coasts an' makin' do

Bad news first, as the articles require. We recover only 64.1% of the lead shot we fire. Two
barrels of careenin' scrapin's, some 280 kg, went into the water off the Port Royal cays in April
2025 when a lighter swamped. Bilge was let go inside the league at Ambon in November 2024. Rats
from Gilde hulls were found ashore twice. The Port Royal warehouse still holds old tortoiseshell
no standin' order of ours would let aboard today. And the E3 water figures are derived, not
measured.

This is the Neederlandsch Schuijnvaerders Gilde N.V.'s account, for FY2025, the reportin' year
102024-092025, against FY2024, the base year 102023-092024, of what fouls the water an' the air
(pollution, ESRS E2), fresh water an' the sea's bounty (water and marine resources, E3), the
livin' coasts an' forests (biodiversity and ecosystems, E4) and makin' do an' makin' over
(resource use and circular economy, E5). It covers a fleet of 25 wooden ships and 5 chambers of
operation and rests on [the E1 activity data](../data/e1-activity-data.csv), the careenin'
records, the cooper's book and the boatswain's rope tally. E2, E4 and E5 were scored material
and E3 partially material in the reckonin' o' what matters (double materiality assessment).

## E2 Pollution — what fouls the water an' the air

### SBM-3 Impacts, risks an' opportunities

Three impacts, all ours and all actual. **The tar kilns**: what does not stay in the pot goes
into the air over a workin' port, which Vlissingen's folk raise at every parley. **Lead**: we fire
lead shot in boardin' practice and lay sheet lead where the shipworm is worst, and lead not
recovered does not go away. **Bilge, ballast an' scrapin's**: a hull makes water carryin' tar,
tallow and filth; ballast is shifted at every careen; and growth scraped off a bottom is weed,
barnacle and old tar. No risk to the purse follows at our scale — no permit, no charge per tonne,
no fine — and covered kilns cut smoke and cost together.

### E2-1 Policies

The standin' orders below are written out in full as [POL-E2 The Clean Wake Order](../policies/e2-pollution.md), adopted by the Kermis Council of October 2026, with their scope, accountability an' the actions planned to FY2030.

Three standin' orders of the Raad van Kapiteins bind every hull and chamber, with no exemption
granted in FY2025. Each Harbourmaster is accountable at their hard, the Quartermaster-General
across the Gilde. We buy no chemical beyond those in E2-5.

- **On tar kilns.** Boiled under a hood of oak and tile, never in the open; kettles sized to the
  work; never banked overnight; skimmin's carried ashore in barrels. Every kettle fired is
  entered in the careenin' record with the weight boiled. This is the order stated in E1-2.
- **On lead shot.** Practice fired into a butt of sand or a hulk's side, never over open water;
  the butt turned and sifted after every practice day; shot recast at the chamber.
- **On bilge an' ballast.** *No bilge pumped and no ballast shifted within a league of a reef or
  a harbour.* Outside it, bilge goes over once the well is sounded and the carpenter is satisfied
  nothin' but water and the ship's own filth is in it. Ballast is stone and sand only, laid on
  ground the Harbourmaster has marked, never on coral.

### E2-2 Actions in FY2025

- **Hoods over every kiln**, at all 5 chambers of operation. Vlissingen, Port Royal and Ambon,
  which boil 81.1% of our tar, were hooded all year; Baltimore and Sainte-Marie only in September
  2025, so their effect falls in FY2026. Tar and pitch boiled fell from 33.4 t to 29.4 t for the
  same work.
- **Sand butts for musketry** at Port Royal and Baltimore moved practice off open water; recovery
  rose from 52.7% to 64.1%, which is not enough.
- **The barrel rule made plain.** Port Royal lands scrapin's in lidded barrels carried in a boat,
  not a lighter, and weighs them ashore.
- **The well sounded twice a watch**, after the Ambon incident.

Nothin' was undone and no action abandoned.

we careen so often.

### E2-6 Anticipated financial effects

**None material.** No chamber holds a permit or consent carryin' a charge; no pollution levy or
penalty was paid in either year; no remediation provision is held; no asset is impaired. Tar,
lead, pigment and sulphur are a small line in the chandlery, and were it to double it would move
nothing.

## E3 Water an' marine resources — fresh water an' the sea's bounty

### SBM-3 Impacts, risks an' opportunities

Partially material, and here is the part. We draw fresh water at five places on three oceans.
Two — Île Sainte-Marie and Ambon — are island chambers where the water is finite, seasonal and
shared with people who were there first, and that is where our draw weighs; elsewhere it is small
against the town's own. The risk to the purse is real but unquantified: a dry season at
Sainte-Marie delays a retourschip's waterin' and so her sailin' date, and a date missed on the
monsoon is a season lost.

### E3-1 Policies

The standin' orders below are written out in full as [POL-E3 The Water Cask Order](../policies/e3-water-marine.md), adopted by the Kermis Council of October 2026, with their scope, accountability an' the actions planned to FY2030.

- **On casks.** Filled only at a spring, stream or well the chamber holds under a written lease
  or a harbourmaster's permission, never at a village's own drawin' place. At Sainte-Marie this
  is the **fresh-water lease from the village of Ambodifotatra**, renewed each Kermis year, which
  carries the undertakin' that the forest above the intake be left standin', for it is the forest
  that holds the water.
- **On yard water.** Careenin', cooperage, the ropewalk and the ovens draw from the chamber's
  cistern and rainwater catch first, and never in the dry season without the Harbourmaster's entry
  in the log.
- **On the ration.** Four and a half litre a hand a day at sea, twenty-five ashore, never cut for
  economy, only by the surgeon.

### E3-2 Actions in FY2025

Roof catches and two cisterns were built at Chambre de Sainte-Marie and one at Kamar Ambon, so
the loft, cooperage and ropewalk take the rains rather than the stream (60 hands' days, the
yard's own timber and tile). The Ambodifotatra lease was read at the village council in October
2024 before renewal, the channel walked by council and Harbourmaster together. And Sainte-Marie
now lands its own victuals when 220 hands come ashore.

### Marine resources

We **take no marine resource commercially**. **No fish**: nothing caught for sale, and what a
hand takes over the side is eaten at the mess table. **No whale**: no hull has hunted one or
carries the gear; whale oil is *bought*, not taken — 9,982 litre in FY2025 — and is bein' replaced
with oil pressed from fish and rapeseed under the E1-3 action. Our ambergris is found on a beach,
never taken from a whale. **No coral, reef stone or shell**, all refused cargo. **No seabed,
dredgin', aquaculture or salt pans.** Marine resources are therefore **not material** beyond the
whale oil we buy, an input rather than an extraction.

## E4 Biodiversity an' ecosystems — the livin' coasts an' forests

### SBM-3 Impacts, risks an' opportunities

Material, and the most uncomfortable of the four. **Forest, windward of us**: a wooden fleet
eats trees, Zeeland grows no oak, and every rib and plank comes from the Baltic, Norway or the
groves near Ambon — a real impact, in somebody else's forest. **The hards**: five of them, four
on livin' coast. **Exotic materials**: sandalwood, pearl shell, camphor, gum copal, beeswax and
logwood are a trade thick with false provenance, and one wrongly papered bale can condemn a cargo
and a forest with it. **Invasive species**: rats aboard, and seed and snail in dry ballast. The
risk to the purse is the Port Royal mangrove, disclosed in E1-9; the fair wind is the Ambon
nursery.

### E4-1 Transition plan

1. **Plant what we cut.** The Vlissingen yard has planted oak on the polder behind the ropewalk
   since 2019: **2.1 ha and 1,450 trees in FY2025**, **6.8 ha standing**. As E1-7 says, it is
   claimed against nothing and counted as no removal until it can be measured.
2. **Buy from groves, not from forests.** Teak below the waterline of the eastern hulls comes
   from managed groves near Ambon under the grower's mark; oak from Baltic and Norwegian woods
   felled and replanted in turn. The mark is 100% of hull timber from a named and replanted source
   by 2030.
3. **An end to careenin' on livin' reef by 2030.** We careen on sand, mud and shingle and believe
   we careen on no livin' coral; the mark is to prove it, by a survey of every hard that the
   chamber and the local fishery both sign.

### E4-2 Policies

The standin' orders below are written out in full as [POL-E4 The Living Coasts Order](../policies/e4-biodiversity.md), adopted by the Kermis Council of October 2026, with their scope, accountability an' the actions planned to FY2030.

**The refused-cargo list**, from the articles.

- **Protected species and materials**: no ivory, rhino horn, pangolin, shark fin, live coral or
  reef stone, wild-taken timber of a listed species, no newly taken sea-turtle shell. *The awkward
  part first: the Port Royal warehouse still holds old tortoiseshell of documented provenance,
  bought long before the standin' order.* It is **held, not traded**, under seal with its papers;
  no new shell has come aboard in livin' memory. We would rather report a cupboard we are ashamed
  of than quietly empty it.
- **People**, in no condition and under no name: the Sainte-Marie clause of 1697.
- **Arms to either side of a war**: no powder, shot, small arms or guns as freight.
- **Anythin' else the articles bar**: unprovenanced antiquities, goods under sanction, cargo
  whose papers we cannot read, and any the Quartermaster may not inspect before stowage.

**On rats.** Every hull carries a cat and keeps her; hulls are smoked at the careen; casks and
sacks land on a rat guard, never on the beach; every rat traced ashore is logged, reported to the
port, and reported here.

**On ballast.** Dry stone and sand only — we carry no ballast water and so have no ballast-water
vector at all. Ballast is laid on ground the Harbourmaster has marked: river-mouth sand at Ambon,
cay sand at Port Royal, never reef stone, and washed before layin'.

**On the hards.** No careenin' on livin' coral; none at Vlissingen in the seal breedin' months,
at Baltimore before the grey seals have whelped, or at Sainte-Marie in the whale months. The
mangrove behind the Port Royal warehouses is **kept, not cut**, since the storm of 1722 took the
houses that had cut theirs.

### E4-3 Actions in FY2025

**2.1 ha of oak planted** at Vlissingen, 1,450 trees. **All five hards walked, measured and
marked** in the careenin' record — 3.7 ha in all — with Port Royal's bounds re-marked 15 paces
back from the reef edge at the fishery's askin'. **The clove an' nutmeg nursery** behind the Ambon
loft gave seedlings to growers takin' the chamber's contract. **Rat guards issued to all 25
hulls**, and smokin' at the careen made a written step. **Provenance read twice**: every bale of
sandalwood, pearl shell and gum copal on PAC-1 read against the grower's mark before stowage and
again ashore, not once as before.

## E5 Resource use an' circular economy — makin' do an' makin' over

### SBM-3 Impacts, risks an' opportunities

Material, and the one topic where our answer is genuinely old. A wooden ship is very nearly a
circle: built of timber, hemp, canvas, iron, tar and tallow, and when broken up her frames become
knees and fenders, her planks dunnage, her rope oakum, her staves casks, the rest firewood and
ash. Nothin' in her cannot be made again by hand. The impact is positive and we still report the
leak: **44.2% of what comes in is food, and food cannot be made over.** That drags our
circularity down, and it should. The risk is supply; the fair wind is that salvage is cheaper
than purchase and always was.

### E5-1 Policies

The standin' orders below are written out in full as [POL-E5 The Make-Do Order](../policies/e5-resources.md), adopted by the Kermis Council of October 2026, with their scope, accountability an' the actions planned to FY2030.

**The salvage articles**, older than the Standards and read aloud at every Kermis.

- **Every prize timber, cask and rope is reused.** A condemned prize's gear, once the Prize
  Council has ruled and the sale is done, goes to the chamber's yard before the auction: rope to
  the rope loft, staves to the cooper, sound timber to the hard.
- **Oakum is picked from old rope, never from new.** A coil condemned on the boatswain's tally is
  cut, unlaid and picked, and the oakum goes into the seams with the tar; new hemp goes to the
  ropewalk and nowhere else.
- **Sails are patched to the end**, then cut down to a smaller suit, then cut for tarpaulins,
  hatch covers, hammocks and boat covers; the loft's cuttings go to the kiln, not the water.
- **A hull broken up is a stock of timber, not a heap of waste.** The yard's book records every
  frame, knee, plank and bolt out of her and where it went.

### E5-2 Actions in FY2025

The cooper's book and the boatswain's rope tally were brought into the same form as the yard's
timber book, so a stave and a coil can be traced as a frame already could — which is why the
FY2025 outflow figures are firmer. **240 t of hull was broken up** at Vlissingen and Port Royal,
of which 204 t went back into the fleet as knees, fenders, dunnage and plank. Galley and kiln
ash, once tipped at three chambers, now goes to the ropewalk's lye and the oak plantin'.

### E5-6 Anticipated financial effects

**None material, and one savin' we have not counted.** No levy, deposit scheme, landfill charge
or extended-producer obligation reaches the Gilde at any of its 5 chambers of operation. The
375.1 t of secondary material is timber, hemp, canvas and iron we did not buy, and would show as
an avoided line in Scope 3 category 1; we have not put a EUR figure on it. Salvage pays for itself
in the yard's own labour, which is why the articles required it three centuries before anyone
called it circular.

## Method an' evidence

Every figure rests on a paper held in a chamber, in ink, sworn at the Kermis closin' on
30 September and copied to Vlissingen, as the Salacia mappin' describes.

- **The careenin' record** of each of the five hards — tar boiled by kettle, scrapin's weighed
  ashore, ballast shifted, the hard's bounds and survey, timber condemned — carries E2-4, E4-5 and
  the timber line of E5-5, and is the record E1-6 draws its tar from.
- **The cooper's book**, per chamber — casks made and re-coopered, staves out, water casks filled
  and where — carries the yard-water and cask lines in E3-4 and E5-5.
- **The boatswain's rope tally**, per hull — every coil by age, condemned, unlaid and picked —
  carries the hemp lines in E5-4 and E5-5.
- **The gunner's book**, per hull, for shot issued, against the chamber's count of shot recast.
- **The E1 ledgers** for every purchased quantity in E4-5 and E5-4, taken from
  [the E1 activity data](../data/e1-activity-data.csv) unchanged so nothing here drifts from E1.

Four judgements an auditor should see plainly: the E3-4 water figures are derived, not metered,
and return the same total both years for that reason; the volatile losses in E2-4 are an estimate
at stated factors, not counted twice against the tar factor in E1-6; the bilge, ballast and
scrapin' figures are averages by hull class from the soundin' and careenin' books; and the timber
m3 in E4-5 is converted at stated densities, the pine not bein' separated in the ledger. Every
number new to this file has been proposed to canon rather than written into it.
