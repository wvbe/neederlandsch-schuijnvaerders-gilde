# E1 Climate Change — the warmin' o' the seas

Bad news first, as the articles require. The Gilde's smoke went down in the year, but not
because we were clever: it went down because we boiled less tar and carried fewer sheep.
Our Scope 3 intensity **rose**, from 73.1 to 73.5 tCO2e per million tonne-nmi carried,
because we laid a keel. And the largest single line in our own Scope 1 is not the guns and
not the kilns; it is the livestock we carry for fresh meat on the long haul.

This file is the Neederlandsch Schuijnvaerders Gilde N.V.'s account of the warmin' o' the
seas (ESRS E1 climate change) for FY2025, the reportin' year 102024-092025, measured
against FY2024, the base year 102023-092024. The tally it rests on is
[the E1 activity data](../data/e1-activity-data.csv): 115 rows for the reportin' year and
104 for the base year, one row per location, period and activity, across all 5 chambers of
operation and a fleet of 25 hulls. The Gilde burns no fuel oil, buys no electricity, and
owns no engines; what follows is an inventory of firewood, charcoal, tar, black powder,
lamp oil, tallow and livestock, kept in a ledger with a quill, and of the goods the
chandlers windward o' us make on our account.

## E1-1 Transition plan

We have no transition to make from engines, for we never had any. Our founders' Vow of the
Old Ways, carried at the Kermis Council o' 1700 and renewed at every Kermis since, forbids
any gear not known aboard a Dutch ship in 1650, and that is why 88.8% of the energy this
company moves on is wind taken free from the air. The course we have set is therefore not
to change our means but to hold them, and to mend the two places where our own means still
smoke: the tar kilns at the careenin' hards and the galley fires aboard.

The plan has three legs, and the Council has costed each against the yard's own labour:

1. **Hold the Vow.** No engines, no bought power, in any hull or chamber, for as long as
   the Gilde sails. This is a standin' order under the articles, not an aspiration.
2. **Mend the kilns.** Hoods of oak and tile over every tar kiln at all 5 chambers of
   operation by 2030, so that what we boil is boiled once and what rises is caught. The
   first five hoods went up in FY2025.
3. **Mend the fires.** Charcoal burnt at our own yards in place of green wood in every
   galley that can carry it, covered galley hearths, and lamp oil pressed from fish and
   rapeseed rather than taken from whales, wherever the articles allow the substitution.

The marks we steer for are set in E1-4: **110 tCO2e** of fossil Scope 1 by 2030 and
**45 tCO2e** by 2050, against a base year of 185.9 tCO2e. We do not claim net zero. A
wooden ship that cooks for 1,944 seagoing hands will always make some smoke, and we would
rather report the residue than buy it away.

## E1-2 Policies

The Gilde's climate policy is the **Vow of the Old Ways**, recorded in
[the backstory](../lore/backstory.md), section 4. It is older than the Standards by three
centuries, it binds every hull and chamber, it is renewed by the vote of all hands at each
Kermis, and it has teeth: forbidden gear goes over the side and the hand who brought it
aboard forfeits a share. The Quartermaster-General keeps the Vow as she keeps the common
chest, and the Advocaat-Boekhouder reads the tallies back against the ledgers before they
are marked final.

Beneath the Vow sit two standin' orders of the Raad van Kapiteins that bear directly on
this topic:

- **The standin' order on galley fuel.** No green wood in a galley where seasoned wood or
  the yard's own charcoal can be had; hearths covered at sea and in port; the cook's fire
  drawn at the end of the second dog watch unless the surgeon asks otherwise. Every hull's
  galley tally goes into the chamber's ledger by the quarter.
- **The standin' order on tar.** Tar and pitch boiled under a hood, never in the open;
  kettles sized to the work in hand; skimmin's and scrapin's carried ashore in barrels and
  never let go on the ebb. Every kettle fired is entered in the careenin' record with the
  weight boiled.

Both orders apply to all 5 chambers of operation and to every hull enrolled at them. The
Quartermaster-General is accountable for the Vow; each chamber's Harbourmaster is
accountable for the two standin' orders at their hard. No exemption was granted in FY2025.

## E1-3 Actions

What we did in the reportin' year, with the resource each took.

- **Charcoal in place of green wood.** The galleys of the 4 retourschepen, the 6 jachten
  and the 5 interdiction sloops moved part of their cookin' onto charcoal burnt at the
  Gilde's own yards from coppice offcuts. The fleet's galley firewood fell from 396.5 t to
  326.3 t, a fall of 70.2 t, and charcoal rose from 5.7 t to 37.3 t. Resource: the yards'
  own offcuts and 6 charcoal pits, dug and fired by the yard hands at Vlissingen, Port
  Royal and Ambon.
- **Covered tar kilns.** Hoods of oak and tile were built over the tar kilns at all 5
  chambers of operation. Tar and pitch boiled fell from 33.4 t to 29.4 t, a fall of 12.0%,
  for the same careenin' work. Resource: 240 hands' days of yard labour and 14 t of oak
  and tile, charged to the careenin' record.
- **Lamp oil from the press, not the whale.** Where the articles allow it — everywhere but
  the sailmakers' lamps, where the loft's masters hold whale oil burns cleaner for close
  work — lamp oil is now pressed from fish and rapeseed at Sainte-Marie and Vlissingen. Of
  the 9,982 litre of lamp oil burned in FY2025, some 2,400 litre was pressed oil. The whole
  line is still booked at the marine-oil factor, which overstates our smoke; we would
  rather overstate than guess.
- **Sail-plan discipline.** Masters were instructed to work the ship rather than the
  galley: more sail carried at night, fewer hours hove to waitin' on weather, and the
  cook's fire drawn in harbour. Lamp oil fell 8.0% and tallow 5.2% against the base year.
  Resource: none but the watch bill.

Nothin' was undone in the year, and no action was abandoned. The kiln hoods at Baltimore
and Sainte-Marie were raised late, in September 2025, and their effect falls in FY2026.

## E1-4 Targets

Two marks, both set by the Raad van Kapiteins at the Kermis of 2025 against FY2024 as the
base year, and both stated gross, without credits or offsets.

| Metric | Base year FY2024 | FY2025 | 2030 mark | 2050 mark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope 1 absolute, fossil (tCO2e) | 185.9 | 168.0 | 110 | 45 |
| Scope 3 intensity (tCO2e per million tonne-nmi carried) | 73.1 | 73.5 | 60 | 45 |

The Scope 1 mark is a fall of 41% by 2030 and 76% by 2050 against the base year, and it is
to be met by the kilns, the galleys and the lamps, not by buyin' anythin'.

The Scope 3 intensity **rose** in FY2025 and we say so plainly. The cause is the keel laid
on the Schelde hard, which puts 550 tCO2e of yard work into category 2 in a year when no
hull was finished. Set the capital goods aside and the same measure falls from 65.6 to
63.6 tCO2e per million tonne-nmi. The Gilde does not regard that as an excuse, only as an
explanation; a company that builds its own hulls will have lumpy years, and the mark is set
on the whole figure.

Carriage for the year is reckoned at 78 million tonne-nmi (FY2024: 74 million), derived
from the burden of each hull in [the fleet register](../lore/fleet.md), her laden fraction
and her voyage book. That figure be held in canon as `e1.cargo_tonne_nmi_fy2025` an' `e1.cargo_tonne_nmi_fy2024`.

## E1-5 Energy

What we burn and what the wind gives free. Energy is estimated from the quantities in the
activity data at the followin' calorific values, which we state so that any auditor may
redo the sum: firewood, air-dried, **4.0 MWh per tonne**; charcoal **8.0 MWh per tonne**;
tar and pitch **10.0 MWh per tonne**; black powder **0.83 MWh per tonne**; lamp oil and
tallow **10.8 MWh per tonne**, lamp oil reckoned at 0.92 kg per litre.

Wind is estimated as mean propulsive power by hull type times hours under sail:
retourschip 250 kW over 280 days, jacht 110 kW over 240, fluyt 120 kW over 250, interdiction
sloop 60 kW over 200, galjoot 35 kW over 260. Muscle is estimated at 75 W of useful work
per hand for 5 hours a day over 300 days, across 2,294 souls all told.

| Energy source | FY2025 (MWh) | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wind | 18,158 | 88.8% |
| Muscle | 258 | 1.3% |
| Biomass (wood, charcoal) | 1,604 | 7.8% |
| Animal fat an' oils (tallow, lamp oil) | 123 | 0.6% |
| Tar, pitch an' black powder | 299 | 1.5% |
| **Total energy consumption** | **20,442** | **100.0%** |

- **Renewable share: 97.9%** — wind, muscle and biomass. Animal fat, marine oil, tar and
  pitch are counted as non-renewable, conservatively, though a case could be made for the
  first two.
- **Energy intensity: 42.6 MWh per EUR million** of net turnover, on a turnover of
  EUR 480 million.
- **Fossil fuel consumption: none.** No fuel oil, no coal, no gas, no bought power.

### Scope 2 — smoke bought in

| Measure | FY2024 | FY2025 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross Scope 2, location-based (tCO2e) | 0 | 0 |
| Gross Scope 2, market-based (tCO2e) | 0 | 0 |

Both are **0**, and the zero is real rather than an omission. The Gilde purchases no
electricity, no heat, no steam and no coolin', at any of its 5 chambers of operation or
aboard any hull, because the Vow of the Old Ways forbids the gear that would consume them.
The chambers work by daylight, candle and lamp; the ropewalk and the sail loft are worked
by hand. There is accordingly no location-based figure and no market-based figure to
distinguish, no contractual instrument, no residual mix, and no rows in the activity data.

Category 1 is two-thirds provisions: 1,310 t of ship's biscuit, salt beef, peas, rice and
salt fish at 2.5 tCO2e per tonne is 3,275 tCO2e, against 522.0 for oak and teak, 495.6 for
iron fittings, 474.5 for sailcloth and 167.2 for hemp cordage. Feedin' 2,294 souls is the
Gilde's largest climate impact by a long way, which surprised the Council and should not
have.

Category 4 is **0** because the Gilde is the transport: every tonne of timber, hemp, canvas
and salt beef that reaches a chamber comes in a hull of our own, and its smoke is already
in Scope 1. Nothin' is hired in.

The categories not shown are **not material** and carry no figure: 3 because we buy no
fuel or power; 5 because the yards' waste is oakum, firewood and fenders, counted under
category 12; 7 because hands live aboard or in the port and walk; 8 and 13 because we
lease no asset and let none; 9 because carriage is the service we sell, not one bought
after it; 10, 11 and 14 because a voyage is consumed as it is made and there are no
franchises; 15 because the Gilde holds no investment beyond its own chambers.

Category 12 deserves a word. A carrier has no sold product to dispose of, so we book the
breakin'-up of our own hulls here rather than leave the line empty: 180 t of condemned
timber at Vlissingen and 60 t at Port Royal in FY2025, at 0.08 tCO2e per tonne, because
nearly all of it is re-used as knees, fenders, oakum and firewood. If a future Standard
puts that elsewhere, we will move it and say so.

## E1-7 Removals an' credits

**None.** The Gilde made no greenhouse gas removals in either year, holds no removal
projects, and bought, sold and retired **no** carbon credits of any kind. It makes no
claim of carbon neutrality, and it does not intend to buy its way to one; the marks in
E1-4 are to be met by burnin' less.

One thing is worth recordin' against a future year. The yard at Vlissingen has planted oak
on the polder land behind the ropewalk since 2019, a slow crop for future keels. Those
trees are a removal on nobody's books yet: not counted in this reckonin', not claimed
against any target, not sold to anyone. When they are large enough to measure and the
boundary is settled, we will disclose them as what they are.

## E1-8 Internal carbon price

**None.** The Gilde operates no internal carbon price, shadow price or internal levy, and
applies none in its investment decisions. The Council considered one at the Kermis of 2025
and declined it: with fossil Scope 1 of 168.0 tCO2e against a turnover of EUR 480 million,
a shadow price would move no decision that the Vow does not already decide. The position
will be revisited if the Scope 1 mark for 2030 is missed.

## E1-9 Financial effects

**Physical risk — severe, and the one that keeps the Council awake.** Three of our 5
chambers of operation sit in the weather's road: Port Royal, a sand spit in the hurricane
belt that the sea has taken once already; Sainte-Marie, across the Indian Ocean cyclone
track; and Ambon, whose monsoon our whole spice calendar assumes. A fleet under sail cannot
outrun a storm it did not see comin', and we carry no gear that would warn us of one.
Warmer seas mean longer storm seasons, shifted monsoon onset, and more days a retourschip
must lie in the lee — which costs voyages, cargo and, at worst, hulls and hands. Our answer
is seamanship, early sailin' dates, mangrove kept standin' behind the Port Royal
warehouses, and hulls built to be mended anywhere.

**Transition risk — none of note.** A carbon price, a fuel levy, a low-carbon fuel
standard, a port charge on emissions: none of them reaches a company that buys no fuel and
owns no engines. The one transition exposure we can name is upstream, in category 1: if
oak, hemp, canvas and salt beef carry a carbon cost, our chandlers will pass it on. On
FY2025 volumes that exposure is the 4,934.3 tCO2e of category 1, and it is the reason the
Gilde buys from woods that are replanted and herds within a day's cart of the quay.

**Opportunity — a fair wind.** Clients are beginnin' to ask for carriage with no fuel in
it, and we are the only carrier that never had any. Zero-fuel carriage, verifiable from a
ledger four centuries deep, is the Gilde's fair wind, and the Letters of Marque Division
reports the same question from clients accountin' for their own downstream carriage. We
have not quantified the revenue effect for FY2025 and do not pretend to.

## Method an' evidence

Every row in [the activity data](../data/e1-activity-data.csv) rests on a paper held in a
chamber, named in the row's `source_document` field and defined in
[the Salacia mappin'](salacia-mapping.md), section 6: the boekhouder's **ledger page** for
galley fuel, lighting, livestock and purchased goods; the **gunner's book**, kept per hull,
for powder fired; and the **careenin' record** of each chamber's hard for tar and pitch
boiled and for timber condemned. Quantities are the Kermis-year totals, closed on
30 September and sworn at Middelburg.

The hand-off is as section 7 of the mappin' describes it: the ledgers come up the Schelde
by galjoot, a notary's clerk employed under the landsmen's exception keys the tallies into
the platform's activity-data import, and the Advocaat-Boekhouder reads every entry back
against the ledger before it is marked final. Unit standardisation is done by the platform
(kg, tonne, litre, head, pkm, unit); emission-factor matchin' is done on the platform
against the categories named in the E1-6 factor table, one factor per activity, with the
factors and calorific values recorded here so the sum can be redone by hand. Three
judgements are worth flaggin' to an auditor: the tar factor is applied to the
whole mass boiled, because the careenin' record cannot separate the kiln fire from the
boil-off; animal fat and marine oil are carried in the Scope 1 total rather than split out
as biogenic, which overstates the total; and lamp oil pressed from fish and rapeseed is
booked at the whale-oil factor for want of a better one. No figure in this file is
estimated where a tally exists.
