# S1 Own Workforce — our hands

Bad news first, as the articles require. Forty-seven hands took a hurt in the Gilde's service in
FY2025. Fourteen fell from aloft. Nine more were scurvy, a provisioning failure and ours to
answer for. And when the Quartermaster-General laid the pay ledger against the adequate-wage
benchmark of each port we pay from, 96 hands — 4.2% of the muster — still earned less than that
benchmark, after the floor payment had been paid to every one of them. The share system carries
that risk and we have not mended it.

This is the Neederlandsch Schuijnvaerders Gilde N.V.'s account of our hands (ESRS S1 own
workforce) for FY2025, the reportin' year 102024-092025, measured against FY2024, the base year
102023-092024. It rests on [the workforce register](../data/workforce.csv), one row per year,
entity, location, rating group, contract type, gender and region of origin, across a fleet of
25 hulls and 5 chambers of operation.

## S1 SBM-3 Material impacts, risks an' opportunities

The reckonin' o' what matters (the double materiality assessment) found these matters weigh.
Worst first.

**Hurts an' hazardous work — actual negative impact, own operations.** Working aloft in a
seaway, serving muzzle-loading guns and heaving a hull down to scrape her are dangerous acts no
standing order makes safe. The impact is severe in kind — a fall from a topsail yard maims or
kills — though our cases were recoverable and no hand died in either year.

**Share-based pay an' the livin'-wage risk — negative impact an' financial risk.** Every
seagoing hand is paid on shares over a monthly floor payment. In a lean season the share is
small and at some ports the floor is below a share a hand can live on (the local adequate
wage). That harms the hand, and because it drives hands away it is a risk to our purse.

**Collective voice — actual positive impact.** Every hand signs the articles, votes for the
Quartermaster-General one hand one vote, sits in a ship's council that may put its own captain
out, and may call the parley without being shifted or docked for it. Coverage is total, and
this is the strongest positive impact we have on our own people.

**Pressin' — historical negative impact, remediated.** Forced recruitment (forced labour) was
a real practice of this trade and of this company. It was abolished by amendment to the
articles, barred again by the Sainte-Marie clause and by the sixth refusal condition, and no
hand was pressed in FY2025 or FY2024. We report it because a remediated wrong not written
down is a wrong waiting to come back.

**No child labour.** Every hand is grown. Age is attested at enrolment before two witnesses
and checked at the Kermis muster against the muster roll; the youngest hand on the register in
FY2025 was 18. The historical powder-boy rating does not exist here.

## S1-1 Policies

The Gilde has one standing order (policy) for its workforce and it is the whole compact:
[the Articles of Agreement](../lore/articles-of-agreement.md), signed or affirmed by every
enrolled hand before a first watch is stood, read aloud each Kermis year, amendable only by a
vote of all hands. The Quartermaster-General is accountable for all of it.

| Matter | Articles | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Health and safety | 8, 9, 22 | Every hurt entered within the watch it happened, paid before any share |
| Social protection | 8, 9 | Fixed sums for named hurts, proportional for lesser ones, paid within one month |
| Forced labour | 21, refusal condition 6 | No person dealt in, held to labour or carried against their will; pressing abolished; any hand may leave at a voyage's end |
| Child labour | 21 | Every hand grown; no rating open to a person under age |
| Discrimination | 1, 3, 15 | One set of articles and one share ladder for every hand; every sanction after a written hearing |
| Pay | 3, 5, 6, 7 | The ladder 2 : 1.5 : 1.25 : 1 and no other; a guaranteed floor; a hardship chest per chamber |
| Discipline | 14, 15 | Marooning abolished; no hand flogged, cut or struck; three sanctions, each appealable |
| Voice and grievance | 2, 11, 12, 16 | Amendment by vote of all hands; a captain removable by her company; the parley open and protected |

**The ILO core conventions, in plain terms.** We hold to all eight: no forced labour of any
kind, and none in the period (29, 105); no child labour and no rating open to a person under age
(138, 182); one ladder paying the same for the same rating whoever holds it (100, 111); and the
right to organize and bargain collectively, which here is constitutive rather than granted,
since the hands elect the officer who holds the chest and amend the articles by their own vote
(87, 98). No chamber sits where we judge these rights at material risk.

## S1-2 Engagement with own workforce

Engagement is how the company is constituted, not a survey we send. Three channels run all year
and each ends at a named officer.

1. **The vote of all hands.** One hand one vote, at sea and ashore, for the
   Quartermaster-General and on every amendment. None was moved in FY2025.
2. **The Kermis parley.** Each chamber holds an open parley at the year's close where any hand
   may speak to the Harbourmaster and the Quartermaster-General's clerk. The coming year's floor
   payment is set there; the FY2026 floor was raised on the hands' motion.
3. **Ships' councils.** Every hull's company elects her captain for three years and may put her
   out at any time save in chase, and its minute goes to the Quartermaster-General.

The hands' perspectives reach the Raad van Kapiteins at every sitting and De Heeren Vijf each
quarter through the Quartermaster-General, who reports to the Muster with no captain in the
room. No hand is represented through a third party, and we do not claim the vote replaces one.

## S1-3 Remediation an' channels to raise concerns

The parley is our grievance mechanism, open under Article 16 to any hand and to any person
ashore in a port we use. The chamber hears it first, the Quartermaster-General on appeal; it is
entered in the parley book with its outcome; and no hand may be turned out of a ship, docked or
shifted for calling it. The hand who calls the Quartermaster (the whistleblower) is protected
by that clause, and the protection has teeth: a clerk was put out of the Gilde in a prior year
for leaning on a caller.

| Concerns raised by hands | FY2025 | FY2024 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parleys called | 31 | 27 |
| Upheld in whole or in part | 22 | 18 |
| Not upheld | 7 | 8 |
| Open at the year end | 2 | 1 |

Of the 22 upheld, 11 concerned pay or the reckoning of a share and were settled from the chamber
counting house; 6 concerned berths, victuals or watch bills and were settled by the
Harbourmaster; 4 were discrimination complaints; 1 concerned safety aloft and produced the
standing order under S1-4. We have not measured whether hands trust the parley, and we name that
gap.

## S1-4 Actions

**On safety aloft.** After the fourteen falls the Raad carried three measures: no hand goes aloft
in her first season without a named hand on the same yard; footropes and their fastenings are
surveyed at every quarterly breaming and condemned on doubt, not on failure; and every surgeon's
report on a fall names the yard, the watch and the weather. The Vlissingen yard made 240 sets of
tarred safety lines.

**On the floor payment.** The floor was extended in FY2025 to every enrolled hand, including
hands ashore between voyages and hands laid off a hull under careen, taking coverage from 92%
to 100%. The hardship chest was drawn on 64 times. Neither action closed the adequate-wage gap
and we do not present them as if it had.

**On training.** The Vlissingen Harbourmaster took 11 apprentices, within the 8 to 12 he holds
to be the most a yard can teach; the Ambon loft kept 22 sailmakers under instruction; the
Sailing Master taught backstaff, lead and log at both. Training hours per hand rose from 36 to
41. No action here is funded from a line absent from the boekhouder's ledger, and none waits on
a prize.

## S1-8 Collective bargainin' coverage an' social dialogue

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hands covered by collective bargaining | 100% | 100% |
| Hands covered by a workers' representative | 100% | 100% |

Coverage is total in every chamber and hull, and here is why we call it collective bargaining in
substance. There is no trade union and no employers' association at the table. There is a written
instrument — the articles — that sets pay, hours, discipline, compensation for a hurt and the
grievance procedure for every hand; that binds the company as fast as the hand; that no officer
may alter; and that is amended only by a vote of all hands, who also elect and may remove the
officer holding the chest. Terms are bargained collectively, agreed in writing and enforceable:
that is the test and we meet it. What we lack is an external representative body, and our 100%
should be read with that qualification.

## S1-9 Diversity metrics

**Top management.** The ten officers in [the officers' register](../lore/people.md) — the
Captain-General, the presiding Lord of De Heeren Vijf, the Quartermaster-General, the
Advocaat-Boekhouder, the five Harbourmasters and the Sailing Master — were 6 women and 4 men in
both years: 60.0% women. Across the Gilde, women hold 54 of 148 officers' ratings, or 36.5%,
against 42.2% of all hands. That difference is the whole of the gap reported under S1-16.

**Age bands.** Of the ten in top management none is under 30, 3 are between 30 and 50 and 7 are
over 50. Across the muster in FY2025: 428 hands under 30 (18.7%), 1,341 between 30 and 50
(58.5%), 525 over 50 (22.9%). We keep hands long: a strength in a trade learnt by hand, and a
succession risk named to the Raad.

## S1-10 Adequate wages

Here is the worst number in this file. Measured against the local adequate-wage benchmark of
each port a hand is paid at, 214 hands would have earned under it in FY2025 on their share
alone. After the floor payment, 96 still did — 4.2% of all hands, in the chambers where the
benchmark is highest. Nothing in this file closes that gap. Pay has three parts and only the
first is guaranteed.

1. **The floor.** Victuals and water aboard, a berth and lodging ashore between voyages, the
   surgeon's attendance, passage home from wherever the voyage ends, and a monthly floor payment
   set each Kermis at the parley. It is paid prize or no prize and from FY2025 reaches 100% of
   enrolled hands.
2. **The share.** Of a prize, net of costs, 20% goes to the crews; of a freight, 25%. That part
   is divided between the hulls that took part by each hull's parten share, and within each
   hull by Article 3 — 2 for a captain, 1.5 for the Quartermaster-General, 1.25 for an officer,
   1 for every other hand. No bonus, no gift, no purse by favour. The share is variable by
   design and in a lean season it is small.
3. **The hardship chest.** Kept in every chamber out of the chamber fee, its key held by the
   Quartermaster-General, drawn by written request to the Harbourmaster with an appeal within
   fourteen days. It was drawn on 64 times in FY2025 for lean seasons, hurts, deaths in a hand's
   family and hulls laid up.

"No prey, no pay" was the old rule; we have softened it, not abolished it, and we report the
residue as a risk under SBM-3.

## S1-11 Social protection

| Event | Cover under the articles | Coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sickness | Surgeon aboard and ashore; the chest maintains a sick hand | 100% |
| Injury and acquired disability | The Matanzas schedule; berth and rating kept; work ashore at rating | 100% |
| Death in service | EUR 120,000 to the next of kin, before any share | 100% |
| A hull laid up | The floor payment continues; the chest maintains the hand | 100% |
| Old age | The chest and a berth ashore; no funded scheme | 100% |

Every enrolled hand is covered against each event in every country we operate in, and coverage
does not vary by chamber, rating, gender or contract type. The gap an auditor should note is old
age: no funded pension scheme, only the chest and a duty to find an old hand work ashore at her
rating. That is a promise, not a fund.

## S1-12 Persons with disabilities

Article 9 is the whole arrangement: a hand's berth and rating are kept while she mends, and
where she cannot go back to sea the Gilde owes her work ashore at her rating. In FY2025,
34 hands held a rating with a lasting impairment from a hurt — 1.5% of the muster. Nine of them
moved ashore at their rating during the year; the rest kept their berths at sea. We do not ask a
hand to declare a disability and record only what the surgeon records, so this is a floor, not a
count of all disabled hands.

**Hours worked, derived.** The Gilde keeps a watch bill, not a time sheet. From it a seagoing
hand stands 2,600 hours in a Kermis year and a hand at a chamber 1,880, giving 1,944 × 2,600
plus 350 × 1,880, or 5,712,400 hours for FY2025 and 5,656,200 for FY2024. The derivation is an
estimate; the hurt counts are not.

**What the numbers mean.** Hurts fell by 7 and the rate by 14%, and we will not claim the S1-4
measures caused it: the fleet spent fewer days on the careenin' hard. Falls from aloft stay the
severest kind and the hardest to shift. The nine scurvy cases were all on long hauls that missed
a fresh-food call, and are booked against the victuallers, not the sea. The FY2025 Matanzas
payments were one loss of an eye, two losses of a finger and six proportional awards under
Article 9; no limb was lost, and nothing is priced below the schedule — EUR 60,000 for the loss
of a right arm, and downward. Every payment was made within one month of the surgeon's report.

## S1-15 Work-life balance

Every enrolled hand is entitled to leave ashore between voyages and to family-related leave, and
the entitlement does not vary by gender, rating, chamber or contract type: 100% are entitled to
both. Leave ashore is not less than 30 days, with lodging and the floor payment continuing; the
average taken in FY2025 was 47 days. In FY2025, 147 hands took family-related leave for a birth,
a death or the care of kin — 71 women and 76 men. We do not yet measure how many returned to the
same rating, and we name that gap.

The honest qualification is the sea itself. A hand on the Cape run is away for months and no
article shortens that; what the articles guarantee is the leave at the other end.

## S1-16 Remuneration metrics

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gender pay gap (mean, total remuneration) | 0.4% | 0.4% |
| Ratio of the Captain-General's pay to the median hand's | 1.9 : 1 | 1.9 : 1 |

The gap is small because it can only be small. A share is fixed by rating, not by the hand who
holds it: every hand of the same rating on the same hull takes the same share, and Article 3
admits no bonus, gift or purse by favour. The whole 0.4% arises from rating mix — women hold
36.5% of officers' ratings while they are 42.2% of all hands — and closes only as that mix
closes, which is what the 2030 women's mark is for.

The ratio of 1.9 to 1 is the Captain-General's whole remuneration — 2 shares on the ladder plus
the floor every hand receives — against the median hand's 1 share plus that floor. We hold no
options, pay no bonus and tie no cash to a mark. One string runs the other way: a fifth of every
officer's share stays in the common chest until the Quartermaster-General has counted the year's
hurts and De Heeren Vijf have approved the reckonin'.

rather than let it fall out of the record.

## Method an' evidence

Every figure rests on paper held in a chamber and named in
[the Salacia mappin'](salacia-mapping.md), section 6.

- **The muster roll**, kept by the Quartermaster-General: hands by name, office, share, chamber
  of origin and age attested. Source of every headcount, gender, origin and leaver, aggregated
  into [the workforce register](../data/workforce.csv) so that no row names a hand.
- **The surgeon's reports**, one per hurt: the counts, kinds, days lost and ill-health cases,
  entered within the watch it happened as Article 22 requires.
- **The Matanzas ledger**, kept with the common chest: every hurt paid, the sum and the date.
- **The parley book**: every concern raised, its hearing and its outcome.
- **The pay ledger** of each counting house: shares, the floor payment and the chest draws;
  source of the adequate-wage comparison.

Three judgements to flag. Hours worked are derived from the watch bill, not measured. The
disability figure counts only what a surgeon recorded, so it is a floor. And the adequate-wage
comparison uses the benchmark of the port a hand is paid at, not the port she is from, the
harder test. The Harbourmaster certifies each chamber's count, the Advocaat-Boekhouder attests
the addition, and bad news goes up first.
