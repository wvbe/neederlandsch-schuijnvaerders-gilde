# The Six Chambers — where the Gilde keeps its feet dry

The Neederlandsch Schuijnvaerders Gilde N.V. (the Netherlandish Guild of Crooked Sailors)
operated from 5 chambers of operation in the reported year 102024-092025, one in each
cluster of the waters we sail. Since the Kermis Council of October 2025 there be a sixth, at
Vava'u in Tonga, set down last below; it opened after the reported year and carries none of
its figures. Each
chamber (kamer) is a legal entity in its own right under the root entity at Vlissingen, and
each is modelled as a location in the Salacia platform, with the hulls enrolled there as
locations beneath it. Together the chambers employ 350 people ashore; the figure per chamber
is given below and in `../data/bases.csv`. What follows is the logbook account of each
chamber, with a formal summary at the head of every section for the landsman in a hurry.

## Kamer Vlissingen — the seat

**Summary (formal).** Kamer Vlissingen is the seat of the Gilde and the root legal entity,
Neederlandsch Schuijnvaerders Gilde N.V. (Netherlandish Guild of Crooked Sailors, public
company), established 1621 at Vlissingen, Zeeland, NL. It houses the counting house, the
shipyard and the ropewalk, and carries a shore headcount of 120. The Prize Council sits up
the Schelde at Middelburg.

**The place an' the hook.** Vlissingen sits where the Westerschelde meets the sea, an' for
thirty-one years, 1585 to 1616, she was an English Cautionary Town — pledged to Elizabeth
against her loans, garrisoned by redcoats' grandfathers, an' handed back to the Republic in
1616. Her captains had raided the Spanish Main afore 1600 an' knew the work. So when the
Twelve Years' Truce lapsed on 9 April 1621 an' the commissions flowed again, a company o'
Vlissingen skippers an' Middelburg merchants signed articles in a tavern by the Sint
Jacobskerk, an' the Gilde was founded anno 1621. Michiel de Ruyter was six that spring, runnin' the same quays.

**What she does today, with 1600s means.** Everythin' the Gilde owns be built or mended
here. The careenin' hard on the Schelde mud takes a retourschip on her side at low water;
the ropewalk runs 300 paces o' hemp along the old English earthwork; the sail loft hangs over the counting house. The counting house keeps the
ledgers o' all 5 chambers in guilders o' 20 stuivers for the flavour an' in EUR for the
regulators. The Kermis year closes here on 30 September, an' every chamber's totals come up
the Schelde by galjoot to be added an' sworn.

**The folk o' the port.** Vlissingen be a shipbuildin' town still, an' the Gilde be the
one yard left that wants shipwrights who know an adze. We take 8 to 12 apprentices a year
from the town, pay the ropewalk hands the same share as a fluyt's cook, an' hold the
Kermis auction o' prize goods on the Beursplein, which the town likes for the trade an'
tolerates for the noise. The grievance the folk bring most often be the tar smoke from the
kilns in a north-easterly; the parley on that be recorded under S3.

**The livin' coast.** Zeeland grows no oak. Every rib an' plank comes down the Rhine as a
raft or across the sea from Norway an' the Baltic, so our forest be windward o' us an' belongs
to others; the yard keeps a book o' every tree by origin an' buys only from woods that be
felled an' replanted in turn. The Schelde mud below the hard be a seal haul-out an' a
feedin' ground for waders; the yard may not careen in the breedin' months, an' the scrapin's
o' weed an' old tar be carried ashore, never let go on the ebb.

## Chamber of Baltimore — the Atlantic door

**Summary (formal).** The Chamber of Baltimore is the legal entity
Crooked Sailors' Guild of Baltimore Limited (Crooked Sailors' Guild of Baltimore; in
Irish, Gild na Seoltóirí Cama, Dún na Séad), established 1631 at Baltimore, West Cork, IE. Its function is the
Atlantic approaches, victualling and careening, with a shore headcount of 45.

**The place an' the hook.** Baltimore, with Leamcon an' Crookhaven along the coast, was the
North Atlantic's pirate headquarters from about 1605 to 1615; Henry Mainwaring called
Ireland "the nursery and storehouse of pirates" in 1618. Then on 20 June 1631 the Algerine corsairs under Murat Reis the Younger,
born Jan Janszoon o' Haarlem, came in the night an' carried off more than a hundred o' the
village into slavery. The Gilde's chamber was established that same year, when the Lords o'
the day sent two jachten to watch Roaringwater Bay for the rest o' the season an' never
called 'em home. That a Dutchman led the raid be the shame the chamber was built to answer:
the articles forbid pressin' an' the takin' o' any person as prize, an' Baltimore be where
that clause be read aloud each Kermis year.

**What she does today, with 1600s means.** Baltimore be the last fresh water an' salt beef
afore the Atlantic an' the first landfall home. The victuallin' store on the quay salts
beef an' pork from Skibbereen, bakes ship's biscuit in two ovens an' fills the casks from
the Ilen. The careenin' hard be a shingle beach under the castle o' Dún na Séad where a
jacht can be hove down between tides. The chamber also keeps the watch on the approaches
for the Letters of Marque Division: what enters the Channel from the west be noted here
first an' sent on to Vlissingen by galjoot.

**The folk o' the port.** A fishin' an' sailin' village o' a few hundred souls, with the ferries to Sherkin an' Cape Clear. The chamber be the largest
employer in the parish after the fishery; hands come from the Irish-speakin' islands as
much as the town, an' the chamber's papers be posted in English an' Irish both. The folk's
standin' complaint be that the Gilde's hulls take the best moorin's in the regatta week;
the chamber moves 'em out to the bay that week under a written undertakin'.

**The livin' coast.** The Ilen estuary an' Roaringwater Bay be a maze o' islands, kelp
forest an' seal rock, with grey seals whelpin' in autumn an' the gannets o' the Bull Rock
fishin' the approaches. The victuallin' store draws its water from the Ilen above the tide
an' keeps to a measured draw so the river runs; the careenin' beach be scraped into barrels,
not into the bay. Salt beef comes from herds within a day's cart o' the quay.

## Chamber of Port Royal — the cruising ground

**Summary (formal).** The Chamber of Port Royal is the legal entity
Crooked Sailors' Guild (Port Royal) Limited (Crooked Sailors' Guild, Port Royal),
established 1660 at Port Royal, Jamaica, JM. Its function is the Caribbean cruising ground, the prize auction house and the
warehouses, with a shore headcount of 70.

**The place an' the hook.** The English took Jamaica in 1655 an' Port Royal on its spit o'
sand became the buccaneer capital o' the Caribbean, Governor Modyford handin' out
commissions against the Spaniard an' Henry Morgan spendin' 'em. The Gilde opened its chamber
there in 1660 to buy prize goods cheap an' sell 'em dear in Vlissingen. The Treaty o' Madrid
in 1670 ended the commissions, an' the Gilde's chamber be the one house that kept its
papers in order through the change, which be why it still stands. On 7 June 1692 the
earthquake put two-thirds o' the town under the harbour in the space o' a few minutes. The
chamber's counting house went with it; the ledgers were fished up an' dried, an' the chamber
was rebuilt on the spit, on timber piles this time, where it be yet. That be the origin o'
our standin' order that every chamber's ledgers be copied to Vlissingen twice a year.

**What she does today, with 1600s means.** Port Royal be the prize auction house o' the
whole Gilde. A prize condemned by the Prize Council at Middelburg an' lyin' in the Caribbean
be sold here under the bell, in the open, with the Advocaat-Boekhouder's clerk at the
table an' the client's agent beside him. The warehouses hold indigo, cochineal, logwood,
tortoiseshell an' the client's seized cargo under seal. The careenin' be done on the cays
across the harbour where the buccaneers did it. Two interdiction sloops an' two jachten be
enrolled here for the Letters of Marque Division's Caribbean work.

**The folk o' the port.** Port Royal today be a fishin' village at the end o' the
Palisadoes road, with Kingston across the water. The chamber hires its warehouse hands,
coopers an' boatmen from the village an' the auction brings the Kingston traders down the
spit six times a year, which the village likes. The complaint recorded most often be the
sloops' powder practice on the far side o' the cays; the chamber now gives the fishermen the
days in writin' a week ahead.

**The livin' coast.** The harbour be ringed with mangrove an' the outer edge o' the spit be
coral reef, both o' 'em the fishery's nursery an' the town's only wall against the next
hurricane. The chamber careens on the sand o' the cays, never on the reef; no ballast
stone be shifted onto coral; an' the mangrove behind the warehouses be kept, not cut, by
order o' the chamber since the storm o' 1722 took the houses that had cut theirs.

## Chambre de Sainte-Marie — the watering place

**Summary (formal).** The Chambre de Sainte-Marie is the legal entity
Guilde des Navigateurs Obliques de Sainte-Marie SARL (Guild of Oblique Navigators of
Sainte-Marie; in Malagasy, Fikambanan'ny Tantsambo Mitongilana), established 1691 at Île Sainte-Marie (Nosy
Boraha), Madagascar, MG. Its function is Indian Ocean victualling, careening and fresh
water, with a shore headcount of 50.

**The place an' the hook.** In 1691 one Adam Baldridge o' New York set up a fortified post
on the islet in the bay o' Sainte-Marie an' sold beef, rum an' powder to every ship on the
pirate round, supplied by Frederick Philipse's sloops out o' New York. The Gilde's ships
watered there from the first season, an' the chamber's founding papers be dated 1691. In
1697 the Malagasy o' the island rose an' burned the post after Baldridge had sold some o'
their own people into slavery. The Gilde's factor was ashore that week an' was not harmed,
because the Gilde had not been in that trade; the crews took the lesson home, an' the clause
o' our articles that forbids forced labour an' any dealin' in persons be dated from that
year an' named for this island.

**What she does today, with 1600s means.** Sainte-Marie be the careenin' an' waterin' place
o' the Indian Ocean run. Retourschepen bound for Ambon an' home be hove down on the sand o'
the inner bay, their casks filled from the streams that come down out o' the forest, an'
their sick set ashore to mend. The chamber keeps a store o' rice, zebu beef an' the island's
own limes, a small ropewalk for repairs, an' the fresh-water rights it rents from the
village o' Ambodifotatra by a written lease renewed each Kermis year.

**The folk o' the port.** The island's people be Malagasy fishers an' farmers, with clove an' vanilla gardens for cash. The chamber
employs from the island first an' teaches the ropewalk trade; its hands sit on the village
council for the water lease. The grievance the folk raise be that a retourschip's company o' 220
set ashore for a fortnight drink the bay dry o' rum an' the market o' fish; the chamber now
lands its own provisions an' fixes the days with the village.

**The livin' coast.** Sainte-Marie be forest, stream an' reef, an' from July to September
the humpback whales come into the channel to calve. No hull be careened or fired for
practice in the whale months; the boats go round the calves, not through 'em. The
chamber's water be drawn from streams above the village, by a measured channel, an' the
forest above the intake be left standin' under the lease, for it be the forest that holds
the water.

## Kamar Ambon — the spice door

**Summary (formal).** Kamar Ambon is the legal entity PT Gilda Pelayar Serong Ambon (Guild
of Crooked Sailors of Ambon, limited company), established 1623 at Ambon, Moluccas, ID.
Its function is the spice islands trade, the Pacific gateway and the sail loft, with a
shore headcount of 65.

**The place an' the hook.** The VOC took the fort at Ambon from the Portuguese in 1605 an'
made it the seat o' its clove monopoly, an' in 1623 its governor tortured an' executed the
English merchants o' the town on a charge o' plot. That be the Ambon affair, an' the Gilde's
chamber was established there in the same dark year, by Zeeland skippers who wanted cloves
without the Company's licence an' found the Ambonese willin' to sell to anyone who paid.
We do not dress that year up. Our articles say the Gilde judges cargo an' never a man's
life, an' the Prize Council's oath repeats it; the chamber's clerks will tell ye the oath
was written with Ambon in mind.

**What she does today, with 1600s means.** Ambon be the Gilde's sail loft. Every suit o'
sails in the fleet be cut here from canvas brought out as cargo, for the loft has the light,
the space an' the sailmakers, three generations o' 'em. The chamber buys cloves, nutmeg an'
mace from Ambon, Banda an' Ternate through the growers' own agents, warehouses 'em on the
bay an' loads 'em into fluyts for the retourschip at Sainte-Marie or straight for home. It
be also the gateway to the Pacific islands trade, where the jachten carry sandalwood,
pearl shell an' the Gilde's letters.

**The folk o' the port.** Ambon be a city o' fishers, clove farmers, clerks an' choirs,
Christian an' Muslim on the one bay. The sailmakers be Ambonese, the loft be the chamber's school, an' the spice be bought at a price set by the
season's council with the growers, not in Vlissingen. The complaint recorded most often be
the warehouse's claim on the waterfront; the chamber has given back the fish market's
frontage an' holds the rest under a lease the city can read.

**The livin' coast.** The Company once cut down every clove tree outside its licensed
gardens to hold the price. The Gilde plants; the chamber keeps a nursery o' clove an'
nutmeg seedlings behind the loft an' gives 'em to growers who take its contract. Ambon Bay
be coral to the shore, an' the chamber's ballast be sand from the river mouth, never reef
stone; the fluyts anchor on the marked sand, an' the loft's cuttings o' canvas an' tarred
twine go to the kiln, not the water.

## Chamber of Vava'u — the far harbour

**Summary (formal).** The Chamber of Vava'u is the legal entity
Crooked Sailors' Guild (Vava'u) Limited (Crooked Sailors' Guild, Vava'u), established 2025
at Neiafu, Vava'u, TO, by vote of the Kermis Council in October 2025. Its function is the Pacific islands run:
harbour, victualling and fresh water at the far end of route PAC-1. It opened after the
close of the reported year 102024-092025, lies outside the FY2025 reporting boundary, and
carries no figure in it. Its shore headcount is first counted in the first reporting year
that includes it.

**The place an' the hook.** For two generations the Gilde's jachten have worked the Pacific
run out o' Ambon, anchorin' off the reef an' rowin' the goods ashore for island folk who
wanted a carrier with no wire an' no smoke. Ninety days out an' ninety home, with nowhere
o' our own at the far end. At the Kermis Council o' October 2025 the hands voted the run a
harbour: Neiafu, on the deep, sheltered water the charts call the Port of Refuge, in the
Kingdom o' Tonga. A sixth ring was cut into the arms that same month. It be the first
chamber opened in three hundred an' thirty-four years, an' the first the Gilde ever opened
by vote o' all hands rather than by a skipper's nerve.

**What she does today, with 1600s means.** Vava'u be the waterin' an' victuallin' place at
the far end o' PAC-1, east o' Fiji, where the hulls o' that run fill their casks, take on
fresh food an' lie snug afore the long beat home. The chamber holds its quay by lease, as
the law o' the Kingdom requires, for no foot o' Tongan soil may be sold. No hull be enrolled
here yet; the hulls o' the run keep their enrolment at Ambon, an' until De Heeren Vijf
appoint the chamber's own Harbourmaster, Ambon's Harbourmaster directs it.

**The folk o' the port.** The chamber hires from Vava'u first an' posts its papers in
Tongan an' English both. Its first open parley be set for the close o' the Kermis year
2026, an' what the folk o' the port bring to it will be recorded under S3 like every
other chamber's.

**The livin' coast.** Vava'u be islands, reef an' deep channel, an' from July to October
the humpback whales come into its waters to calve. The Sainte-Marie rule holds here: no
hull careened or fired for practice in the whale months, an' the boats go round the calves.
No hull careens on the reef, an' none lies in the harbour through the cyclone season,
November to April, when the run be home.
