# The Fleet — 25 hulls an' who sails 'em

The Gilde sails a fleet of 25 wooden ships, crewed by 1,944 seagoing hands and grouped by
type as canon records them: 4 retourschepen (East Indiaman) of 220 hands each, 6 jachten
(light frigate) of 90, 8 fluyts of 18, 5 interdiction sloops or barks of 70, and 2 galjoots
(advice boat) of 15. Every hull is company-owned, enrolled at one of the five chambers and
modelled in the Salacia platform as a location under that chamber's legal entity. Each
ship's company holds a minority "parten" interest in her hull, stated below as a fraction,
and is paid out of her earnings as the crew share. The register in `../data/fleet.csv` is
the machine layer of this file; the hull marks NSG-01 to NSG-25 are the keys.

A word on the hulls afore the list. A wooden ship lives forty to sixty years if she be
careened, caulked an' loved, an' then she be broken up an' her name be given to the next
keel laid on the Schelde hard. So every launch year below be the year o' the *present* hull,
between 1968 an' 2021; the names be older, some o' 'em by four centuries. The guns be
muzzle-loadin' cannon o' iron an' bronze, served with powder an' match, an' the tally o'
powder burnt goes into the gunner's book an' from there into Scope 1. No hull carries a
thing the articles forbid, an' the Quartermaster o' each hull swears to it at the Kermis
muster.

## Retourschepen — the long haul

Four great ships o' 220 hands apiece, the East Indiaman o' the Gilde: three-masted,
deep-waisted, slow to turn an' near impossible to stop. They carry the valuables — spices,
bullion under seal, dyestuffs, ambergris, tortoiseshell — between Vlissingen an' the far
chambers, an' they carry the soldiers' share o' the crew, for a retourschip's hands be half
sailors an' half the men who board an' hold. By the type rule o' the brand book they be
named for the towns an' waters o' Zeeland an' Holland in the old spellin', whichever
chamber enrols 'em.

| Hull mark | Name | Chamber of enrolment | Launched | Tons | Guns | Role | Typical route | Parten share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NSG-01 | *Zierickzee* | Kamer Vlissingen | 1978 | 820 | 28 | cargo | Vlissingen - Cape of Good Hope - Sainte-Marie - Ambon and home | 1/16 |
| NSG-02 | *Ter Veere* | Kamer Vlissingen | 1989 | 780 | 26 | cargo | Vlissingen - Baltimore - Port Royal and home | 1/16 |
| NSG-03 | *Middelburgh* | Kamer Vlissingen | 2003 | 850 | 30 | cargo | Vlissingen - Cape of Good Hope - Sainte-Marie - Ambon and home | 1/16 |
| NSG-04 | *De Honte* | Kamar Ambon | 1996 | 760 | 24 | cargo | Ambon - Banda - Ternate - Sainte-Marie, and the Pacific islands in season | 1/16 |

- **Zierickzee** (NSG-01). Flagship o' the Ambon run since the first Zierickzee o' 1624; the present hull be the seventh o' the name, launched off the Schelde hard in 1978, an' still the fastest o' the four in a followin' sea.
- **Ter Veere** (NSG-02). The Caribbean valuables ship. Her strongroom be a cask-lined hold under the great cabin with three locks an' three keyholders: the Cap'n, the Quartermaster an' the client's agent.
- **Middelburgh** (NSG-03). Named for the town that condemns our prizes, which the hands find funny. The heaviest o' the retourschepen an' the only one carryin' a surgeon's mate as well as a surgeon.
- **De Honte** (NSG-04). Enrolled at Ambon an' keeps to the eastern seas. Named for the old mouth o' the Schelde by the type rule, though her sailmakers call her Si Honte; rebuilt at Ambon with teak below the waterline.

## Jachten — the fast an' the fierce

Six jachten o' 90 hands, the light frigate o' the fleet: two-masted or three, fine-lined,
fast in a breeze an' armed enough to make a mark think twice. They escort the retourschepen
out an' home, carry the cargo that must not wait — cochineal, indigo, letters, coin — an'
stop marks for the Letters of Marque Division when a sloop be not to hand. Named for birds
o' the sea an' birds o' prey in the tongue o' their chamber.

| Hull mark | Name | Chamber of enrolment | Launched | Tons | Guns | Role | Typical route | Parten share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NSG-05 | *De Stormvoghel* | Kamer Vlissingen | 1984 | 240 | 20 | mixed | North Sea and Channel escort; Vlissingen - Baltimore | 1/8 |
| NSG-06 | *The Gannet* | Chamber of Baltimore | 1992 | 210 | 18 | mixed | Atlantic approaches; Baltimore - Vlissingen; escort of homebound retourschepen | 1/8 |
| NSG-07 | *The Frigate Bird* | Chamber of Port Royal | 2001 | 230 | 20 | mixed | Caribbean cruising ground; Port Royal - Curacao - the Havana approaches | 1/8 |
| NSG-08 | *The Pelican* | Chamber of Port Royal | 1971 | 200 | 16 | mixed | Port Royal - Cartagena - Vlissingen fast cargo (cochineal, indigo) | 1/8 |
| NSG-09 | *Le Paille-en-queue* | Chambre de Sainte-Marie | 2010 | 220 | 18 | mixed | Indian Ocean; Sainte-Marie - Mozambique Channel - Mauritius - the Cape | 1/8 |
| NSG-10 | *Elang Laut* | Kamar Ambon | 2016 | 250 | 20 | mixed | Ambon - the Pacific islands (sandalwood, pearl shell); Banda Sea escort | 1/8 |

- **De Stormvoghel** (NSG-05). The Council's own jacht, an' the Captain-General's when he sails. Escorts the retourschepen out past Ushant an' comes home with the west-country letters.
- **The Gannet** (NSG-06). Watches the approaches from Roaringwater Bay to the Scillies. Her lookouts be paid a stuiver a sightin' an' have the best eyes in the Gilde.
- **The Frigate Bird** (NSG-07). Fast, long-winged an' never home empty. The Letters of Marque Division's first choice for stoppin' a mark an' readin' her papers.
- **The Pelican** (NSG-08). Oldest jacht on the register an' the slowest, but she carries the dyestuffs that pay the Caribbean chamber's rent. Her company voted twice to keep her rather than take a new hull.
- **Le Paille-en-queue** (NSG-09). Named for the tropicbird with the long white tail. Guards the waterin' place an' runs the Mozambique Channel for the Division under the client's commissie.
- **Elang Laut** (NSG-10). The sea eagle. Built at Vlissingen to Ambon's order an' sailed out by her own sailmakers; carries the Gilde's letters to the Pacific islands an' comes back with sandalwood.

## Fluyts — the patient carriers

Eight fluyts o' 18 hands, the Gilde's bread an' butter an' the ships the Zeeuws are
proudest of: pear-bellied, cheap to man, deep in the hold an' narrow on the deck, built to
carry the most for the fewest hands. They fetch the timber, hemp an' tar the yard needs,
the beef an' biscuit the crews eat, an' the bulk spices an' materials that be the Gilde's
steady trade. Lightly armed an' never sent to board; their defence be to look poor. Named
for the virtues in old Dutch, with a straight face.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| NSG-12 | *Voorsichticheyt* | Kamer Vlissingen | 1988 | 380 | 6 | cargo | Vlissingen - Norway (timber) - Vlissingen | 1/16 |
| NSG-13 | *Trouwe* | Kamer Vlissingen | 1999 | 420 | 8 | cargo | Vlissingen - Port Royal bulk (logwood, cacao, salt); calls at Baltimore | 1/16 |
| NSG-14 | *Rechtvaerdicheyt* | Chamber of Baltimore | 2005 | 350 | 4 | cargo | Baltimore - Skibbereen - Vlissingen (salt beef, biscuit, casks) | 1/16 |
| NSG-15 | *Eendraght* | Chamber of Port Royal | 1968 | 360 | 6 | cargo | Port Royal - the cays - the Jamaican coast; warehouse tender and prize-goods carrier | 1/16 |
| NSG-16 | *Matigheyt* | Chambre de Sainte-Marie | 1995 | 340 | 4 | cargo | Sainte-Marie - the Madagascar mainland (rice, zebu, limes) - Mauritius | 1/16 |
| NSG-17 | *Neersticheyt* | Kamar Ambon | 1982 | 440 | 8 | cargo | Ambon - Banda - Ternate spice run; feeder to the retourschepen | 1/16 |
| NSG-18 | *Spaersaemheyt* | Kamar Ambon | 2014 | 430 | 6 | cargo | Ambon - Sainte-Marie (spices west; canvas and iron east) | 1/16 |

- **Gheduld** (NSG-11). Patience. Fetches the oak, hemp an' Stockholm tar the yard lives on; 18 hands an' a cat, an' she has never once been late for the Kermis closin'.
- **Voorsichticheyt** (NSG-12). Prudence. Her skipper reefs afore the squall shows an' has lost no spar in twenty years; the yard's apprentices learn the ropes aboard her.
- **Trouwe** (NSG-13). Fidelity. The Atlantic fluyt, deep an' slow; carries logwood an' salt home an' biscuit an' cordage out, an' feeds the Baltimore store on the way.
- **Rechtvaerdicheyt** (NSG-14). Justice. The victuallin' fluyt; every barrel o' beef in the Gilde has been aboard her, an' the hands hold the name be a joke on the cook.
- **Eendraght** (NSG-15). Concord. Oldest hull on the register. Lays the prize goods along the wharf at auction an' carries sealed cargo from the sloops to the warehouse without breakin' bulk.
- **Matigheyt** (NSG-16). Temperance. Carries rice an' beef to the waterin' place from the mainland an' rum away from it, which the hands find fittin' for the name.
- **Neersticheyt** (NSG-17). Diligence. The clove fluyt; loads at Banda an' Ternate an' tranships to the retourschip at Ambon, as the first o' the name did from 1631.
- **Spaersaemheyt** (NSG-18). Thrift. The canvas ship; carries sailcloth to the loft eastbound an' cloves an' nutmeg west to the retourschip at Sainte-Marie. Her boekhouder counts nails.

## Interdiction sloops — the boarders

Five sloops or barks o' 70 hands, the workin' end o' the Letters of Marque Division:
single-masted or two, shallow, quick, with long sweeps for a calm an' a boardin' party that
be most o' the company. They carry a client's registered commissie, stop the mark, examine
her papers an' crew by written interrogatory, seal the cargo without breakin' bulk an' bring
her to the nearest chamber for the Prize Council at Middelburg to judge. Their crews hold
the largest parten share in the fleet, 3/16, for theirs be the work that earns the prize
money an' bears the weather. Named for weather an' sea states in the tongue o' their
chamber.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NSG-19 | *The Sou'wester* | Chamber of Baltimore | 2008 | 110 | 10 | interdiction | Atlantic approaches, Roaringwater Bay to the Channel mouth, under commissie | 3/16 |
| NSG-20 | *Squall* | Chamber of Port Royal | 1990 | 100 | 10 | interdiction | Caribbean cruising ground; the Windward Passage and the Jamaica Channel | 3/16 |
| NSG-21 | *Norther* | Chamber of Port Royal | 2019 | 120 | 12 | interdiction | Caribbean cruising ground; the Yucatan Channel and the Gulf approaches | 3/16 |
| NSG-22 | *La Rafale* | Chambre de Sainte-Marie | 1997 | 105 | 10 | interdiction | Mozambique Channel and the approaches to Sainte-Marie, under commissie | 3/16 |
| NSG-23 | *Angin Ribut* | Kamar Ambon | 2012 | 115 | 12 | interdiction | Banda Sea and the Molucca passages, under commissie | 3/16 |

- **The Sou'wester** (NSG-19). Named for the wind that brings the marks in. Boards in the Channel mouth under a registered commissie an' brings 'em to Baltimore under seal for the Prize Council.
- **Squall** (NSG-20). The Division's Caribbean workhorse. Her boardin' party drills with blunted cutlasses an' has never lost a hand, nor taken a life, in a boardin'.
- **Norther** (NSG-21). The cold north wind o' the Gulf. Longest sweeps an' lightest draught o' the five, built for the Yucatan shoals where the marks hide.
- **La Rafale** (NSG-22). The gust. Works the Mozambique Channel for the Division an' reads papers in French, Portuguese an' Arabic; touches no pilgrim ship, by the articles.
- **Angin Ribut** (NSG-23). The storm wind. Guards the spice run through the Molucca passages an' serves the Division's eastern commissions; her boardin' party be Ambonese an' Zeeuws in equal parts.

## Galjoots — the messengers

Two galjoots o' 15 hands, the advice boat o' the Gilde: small, handy, rounded fore an' aft,
built to run letters, totals an' water along a coast an' up a river. They carry no cargo to
speak of an' only enough guns to be taken seriously by a fishin' boat. Named for messengers
an' haste in the tongue o' their chamber.

| Hull mark | Name | Chamber of enrolment | Launched | Tons | Guns | Role | Typical route | Parten share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NSG-24 | *De Bode* | Kamer Vlissingen | 2021 | 70 | 2 | dispatches | Vlissingen - Middelburg - Baltimore; the Kermis-year totals run | 1/8 |
| NSG-25 | *An Teachtaire* | Chamber of Baltimore | 1979 | 65 | 2 | dispatches | Baltimore - the Irish and Cornish coasts - the Channel; coastal supply and dispatches | 1/8 |

- **De Bode** (NSG-24). The messenger. Carries the chambers' totals up the Schelde to Middelburg at the Kermis closin' an' the Council's letters down. Newest hull on the register.
- **An Teachtaire** (NSG-25). The messenger, in Irish. Runs water, biscuit an' letters along the coast an' meets the homebound retourschepen off the Scillies with the mails.
