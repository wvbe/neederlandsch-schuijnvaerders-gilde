# The Articles of Agreement o' the Neederlandsch Schuijnvaerders Gilde

## Preamble

These Articles were first signed at Vlissingen on 9 April 1621, when the Gilde was founded
anno 1621, and they have been amended by vote of all hands nine times since. They are the
whole of the compact between the Gilde and the people who sail and serve it: what a hand is
owed, what a hand owes, who may be elected and who may be put out, what shall be done when
a hand is hurt, what the Gilde will not do for any client at any price, and what shall be
written down. Every enrolled hand, at sea and ashore, and every officer named in the
register, signs or affirms them on enrolment and hears them read at the close of each
Kermis year. Where a clause carries a note in the margin, that note names the record it
comes from or marks it invented; the record itself is set out in
[the research dossier](research/real-pirate-practices.md). No clause of these Articles may
be read to permit what Article 20 forbids.

## Part I — Of the compact

**1.** Every hand signs these Articles afore a first watch be stood, an' none may be put
to work who has not. A hand who cannot write makes a mark afore two witnesses, an' the mark
binds the Gilde as fast as a signature binds the hand.
*Roberts' articles, 1721; Phillips' articles, 1723; Exquemelin's chasse-partie, 1678.*

**2.** These Articles be amended by a vote o' all hands, one hand one vote, on a motion
carried in the Raad van Kapiteins an' approved by De Heeren Vijf. The date o' every
amendment stands in the margin o' the copy at Vlissingen an' be never struck out, so that
what the Gilde has repented o' can still be read.
*Invented; the record has no amendment procedure.*

## Part II — Of shares an' o' pay

**3.** The share ladder be: Captain 2 shares; Quartermaster 1.5; officers 1.25; every other
hand 1. There be no other ladder, no bonus, no gift an' no purse by favour.
*Roberts' articles, 1721 (2 : 1.5 : 1.25 : 1).*

**4.** The company o' each hull holds a collective parten interest in her, stated as a
fraction in the fleet register, an' be paid out o' her earnings as the crew share. A hull
be never renamed nor sold out o' the Gilde without a vote o' her company.
*Dutch partenrederij, crossed with company ownership; the crossing be invented.*

**5.** "No prey, no pay" be the old rule an' the Gilde has softened it, an' says so
plainly. Every enrolled hand be guaranteed victuallin' an' lodgin' aboard or ashore, foul
season an' fair, whether a prize be taken or none: three meals, a dry berth, fresh water,
slops an' the surgeon. The share sits on top o' that floor. We do not pretend the floor be
a livin' wage in every port we touch; it be not, the risk be reported as a risk under S1,
an' the hardship chest in Article 6 be the part o' it we have mended so far.
*Exquemelin, 1678, softened; the softening an' the honest disclosure be invented.*

**6.** Each chamber keeps a hardship chest out o' the chamber fee, an' the
Quartermaster-General keeps its key with the common chest. A hand in want through a lean
season, a hurt, a death in the family or a hull laid up may draw on it by written request to
the chamber's Harbourmaster, an' a refusal be appealable to the Quartermaster-General
within fourteen days.
*Invented.*

**7.** Afore any share be counted, the common stock pays the victuallin', the surgeon's
chest an' the carpenter's stores. What be left be the sum the ladder divides.
*Exquemelin's chasse-partie, 1678.*

## Part III — Of hurts: the Matanzas schedule

**8.** A hand hurt in the Gilde's service be paid out o' the common chest by the schedule
below, afore any share be divided to any person. The schedule be called the **Matanzas
schedule** an' be dated 1628, for it was first agreed on the division o'
Piet Heyn's silver fleet, Matanzas Bay, 8 September 1628. The amounts be restated in EUR an' be reviewed by
all hands every seven years.

| hurt | amount |
| --- | --- |
| loss of the right arm | EUR 60,000 |
| loss of the left arm | EUR 50,000 |
| loss of the right leg | EUR 50,000 |
| loss of the left leg | EUR 40,000 |
| loss of an eye | EUR 10,000 |
| loss of a finger | EUR 10,000 |
| death in service, to the next o' kin | EUR 120,000 |

*Exquemelin, 1678 (600 / 500 / 500 / 400 / 100 / 100 pieces of eight), restated in EUR; the
EUR amounts an' the death-in-service line be invented.*

**9.** A lesser hurt be paid in proportion by the Quartermaster-General, an' the proportion
be entered in the parley book with the surgeon's word beside it. Payment be due within one
month o' the surgeon's report. A hand's berth an' rating be kept while he mends, an' where
he cannot go back to sea the Gilde owes him work ashore at his rating.
*Roberts' articles, 1721 ("proportionably for lesser hurts"); the berth an' the shore work
be invented.*

**10.** The old schedule offered payment in enslaved persons in place o' coin. That line be
struck out o' these Articles for ever an' be recorded as the wrong it was.
*Exquemelin, 1678, repudiated.*

## Part IV — Of votes

**11.** A ship's company elects her captain, an' the same company may put her out by a vote
at any time, save in chase or in battle, when her word stands unquestioned till the deck be
clear. A vote to put a captain out be taken by the ship's quartermaster an' minuted.
*Johnson's General History, 1724; the written minute be invented.*

**12.** All hands together elect the Quartermaster-General. She keeps the common chest,
holds the veto on every distribution out o' it, an' none may stand for the office who has
held a command in the three years afore.
*Elected quartermaster as the crew's trustee, Exquemelin an' Johnson; the three-year bar be
invented.*

**13.** The captains sit as the Raad van Kapiteins an' elect a Captain-General from among
themselves. Neither the Raad nor the Captain-General may set aside a vote o' a ship's
company in her own ship's business.
*Buccaneer councils of captains, Morgan's Panama fleet 1670–71.*

## Part V — Of discipline

**14.** Marooning be abolished. The word be kept for the last sanction an' the thing be
gone: no hand be put ashore on any island, no hand be flogged, cut or struck, an' no hand
be punished in his body for anythin' at all.
*Phillips' articles, 1723, abolished.*

**15.** The sanctions be three, in order: a fine, not above one share; loss o' share for a
named voyage; dismissal from the Gilde. Each follows a hearin' afore
the Quartermaster-General, with the hand heard an' the finding written, an' each be appealable to the Raad
van Kapiteins within twenty-eight days.
*Loss of share, Phillips' articles, 1723; the hearin' an' the appeal be invented.*

**16.** The parley be the grievance o' the Gilde. Any hand, an' any person ashore in any
port we use, may call it. It be heard by the chamber first an' by the Quartermaster-General
on appeal, it be entered in the parley book with its outcome, an' no hand be turned out o'
a ship, docked or shifted for callin' it.
*Quartermaster as settler of quarrels, Exquemelin; the protection o' the caller be invented.*

## Part VI — Of prizes an' o' prisoners

**17.** The Letters of Marque Division serves no commission but through these 5 steps, in
this order: the client's letter of marque be registered at Middelburg an' a bond posted;
the mark be stopped an' her papers an' her crew examined under a written interrogatory;
the cargo be sealed an' brought to the nearest chamber without breakin' bulk; the Prize
Council rules good prize or orders restitution, an' the costs o' a failed seizure fall on
the client; the prize be sold at open auction or released against a written ransom bill.
*Dutch Admiralty instruction of 1597 an' the High Court of Admiralty; the client in place of
the state be invented.*

**18.** There be 8 conditions on which the Gilde refuses, an' a refusal needs no leave from
any client: no seizure o' vessels o' the Netherlands, o' the EU, or o' the client's declared
allies; no commission from a client at war with the Netherlands, an' none against a party
under truce; no breakin' bulk nor sale afore adjudication; no seizure without inspection o'
papers, an' clean papers means release with the costs to the client; no work outside the
waters or the term named in the commission; no pressin' o' captured crew; no hostage-takin'
to secure a ransom bill, for a ransom be a bond an' never a body; no attack under a flag o'
truce nor in a neutral harbour.
*Stricter than any historical prize court; the eight conditions be invented.*

**19.** Of the people aboard a mark: not one o' 'em be a prize. No person be pressed, held,
carried off or bargained for. Boardin' parties carry blunted steel an' the arms stay
housed; no life be taken, an' none was taken in the reported year. A mark's crew be fed,
their sick tended by our surgeon, an' they be set ashore or sent on with their papers.
*Forcing of skilled men, an' hostage ransom, both abolished; the abolition be the Gilde's.*

## Part VII — Of the Old Ways

**20.** The Vow of the Old Ways be sworn in 1700 an' binds every hand, every hull an' every
chamber: **Nothing aboard or ashore that was not in use on a Dutch ship in the 1600s**. The
Quartermaster-General enforces it, an' each hull's quartermaster swears to it at the Kermis
muster. Gear that breaks the Vow goes over the side where it be found, an' the hand who
brought it aboard forfeits one share. A second forfeiture be a matter for Article 15.
*Invented; the technology inventory it freezes be the dossier's.*

## Part VIII — Of persons

**21.** The Sainte-Marie clause, sworn in 1697 after the risin' on that island: the Gilde
deals in no person, holds no person to labour, an' carries no person against their will.
Every hand be grown; none under age comes aboard in any rating. Pressin' be abolished an'
be reported as the past wrong it be. Any hand may leave the Gilde at the end o' a voyage
with what be owed 'em paid.
*Sainte-Marie uprising, 1697; the clause be the Gilde's.*

## Part IX — Of the reckonin'

**22.** The reckonin' o' the year be a standin' order o' these Articles an' not a favour to
any regulator. Every hurt taken in the Gilde's service, an' every measure o' wood, tar,
powder an' oil burnt, be tallied per hull an' per chamber within the watch in which it
happened; the Harbourmaster certifies, the Advocaat-Boekhouder adds an' attests, the
Quartermaster-General counts the counted, an' bad news goes up first.
*Invented; built on the ships' an' gunners' books o' the period.*

**23.** The landsmen's exception. The Gilde may keep a notary's clerk ashore, an' that
clerk may carry the fair copy o' the ledgers to the Salacia platform an' enter 'em there.
That be the one act o' the modern world these Articles permit, it be done only through a
landsman's hands, never by a hand o' the Gilde an' never aboard a hull, an' the instruction
to the clerk be written fresh each Kermis year by the Advocaat-Boekhouder.
*Invented.*
