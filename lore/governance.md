# Who Holds the Helm — the governance o' the Gilde

The Neederlandsch Schuijnvaerders Gilde N.V. is governed in two tiers. Below sails the
crews' tier, which elects its own officers and runs the trade; above it sits the chambers'
tier, which appoints, watches and approves. Neither tier may sit in the other's chair. The
arrangement is old: the elected captain and the elected quartermaster come from the ships,
the chamber directors come from the counting house, and the Gilde has kept both because
each holds the other honest. The historical record for both is set out in
[the research dossier](research/real-pirate-practices.md).

This file names the bodies, says how each is filled and how often it sits, and shows where
a matter of sustainability enters each of them. The last section is the machine layer: a
table that gives, for every body and office, the modern role an auditor expects, so that
ESRS 2 GOV-1 to GOV-5 can be answered from this document without guesswork.

## 1. De Heeren Vijf — the supervisory tier

De Heeren Vijf (the Lords Five) are 5 bewindhebbers, one for each chamber. A chamber's
bewindhebber is nominated by that chamber's ships' councils and its shore muster together,
and appointed by De Heeren Vijf sitting, for four years, renewable once and no longer. No
bewindhebber may at the same time hold a command at sea, a Harbourmaster's chair or the
office of Advocaat-Boekhouder; that is what keeps the tier a watch and not a second crew.

The presidency turns about the 5 chambers on a fixed wheel: **Vlissingen three years, each
other chamber one year**. The chamber whose turn it is names one of its own as presiding
Lord for that turn. The title belongs to the office, not to the person who fills it.

De Heeren Vijf sit four times in the Kermis year, once in each quarter, the October sitting
at Vlissingen and the rest where the presidency lies. The Quartermaster-General may call
them within fourteen days at any time and need give no reason beyond "a matter for the
Lords".

Twice a year the same five sit as **the Muster**: the audit session, held on the year's
reckoning in October and on the half-year in April. At the Muster the Advocaat-Boekhouder
lays the figures on the table, the Quartermaster-General is heard, and then heard
again alone, with no captain in the room. What De Heeren Vijf approve at
the October Muster is the reckonin' o' the year (the sustainability statement).

## 2. De Raad van Kapiteins — the management tier

The Raad van Kapiteins (Council of Captains) is the management board. It is every captain
of the fleet of 25, and a captain sits on it because her own ship's company elected her,
not because the Lords chose her. A captain is elected by the ship's council for three years
and may be put out by a vote of the same company at any time, save in chase or in battle,
when her word stands unquestioned until the deck is clear. That is the oldest rule aboard
and the articles keep it whole.

The Raad elects from among its own a **Captain-General**, who presides, for four years,
renewable once. The Captain-General has no vote beyond his own and no power to set aside a
vote of the Raad; he speaks for it, signs for it, and answers to it.

The Raad musters in full twice a year: in October at Vlissingen at the close of the Kermis
year, and in April. Between musters a standing committee of five captains, one enrolled at
each chamber, sits monthly. A captain at sea sends her voice in writing by galjoot, and a
voice so sent counts as if she stood in the room.

The Raad sets the marks (targets) for the year, receives the Quartermaster-General's report
at every sitting, and may set no mark that the Quartermaster-General has not first costed
in wood, tar, powder, oil and hands.

## 3. The Quartermaster-General

The Quartermaster-General is elected by all hands — every enrolled hand, at sea and ashore,
one hand one vote — for three years, renewable twice. No one who has held a command in the
three years before may stand. She keeps the common chest, and no distribution of any kind
leaves it over her veto; the veto may be overridden only by the Raad and De Heeren Vijf
voting the same way in the same quarter, and the override goes in the reckonin'.

She is also the Gilde's sustainability officer and the officer to whom a hand calls when
something is wrong. The parley — our grievance procedure — ends at her table, and the hand
who calls her may not be put out of a ship for calling. She reports to **both** tiers: to
the Raad at every sitting, to De Heeren Vijf at every quarter, and to the Muster alone.

## 4. The Advocaat-Boekhouder

The Advocaat-Boekhouder is secretary of both tiers and chief bookkeeper of the Gilde,
appointed by De Heeren Vijf on the Raad's nomination for five years. She attends both
tiers, drafts their resolutions, keeps the minute book and the register of officers, and
holds no vote in either. Every figure that leaves the Gilde — the tally of a hull, the
totals of a chamber, the reckonin' o' the year — carries her attestation that it agrees
with the ledgers, or it does not leave. Her clerks keep the ledgers at the counting house
at Vlissingen; each chamber's books are copied there twice a year, a standing order since
the Port Royal earthquake of 1692.

## 5. The Prize Council at Middelburg

The Prize Council sits at Middelburg and adjudicates every seizure the Letters of Marque
Division makes: good prize, or restitution with the costs falling on the client. It is
three assessors — one bewindhebber who is not of the chamber that brought the prize in, one
captain who is not of the ship that took her, and the Advocaat-Boekhouder or her deputy —
and the Quartermaster-General has the right to sit with them, to speak and to have her
dissent written into the sentence.

It sits in ordinary session four times a year, and on a seizure within forty days of the
cargo being landed under seal. Its sentences, its refusals and the papers of every stopped
ship are the Gilde's evidence archive, and the conduct disclosures under ESRS G1 are drawn
from them.

## 6. The Harbourmasters

Each chamber's legal entity has one director, called its Harbourmaster. A Harbourmaster is
appointed by De Heeren Vijf on the joint nomination of the chamber's bewindhebber and the
chamber's shore muster, for four years, renewable. She runs the yard, the store, the loft
or the auction house of her chamber, holds its lease and its books, and certifies her
chamber's tallies to the Advocaat-Boekhouder before they are added.

A Harbourmaster's own chamber board sits monthly. All five sit together as the
harbourmasters' muster twice a year, before each full muster of the Raad, to settle what
the chambers will do about the marks the Raad has set.

## 7. How a matter of sustainability reaches each body

Bad news travels first and travels up the same rope as good.

1. **Aboard and ashore.** Every hurt taken in the Gilde's service, and every measure of
   wood, tar, powder and oil burnt, is entered per hull and per chamber as the articles
   require, within the watch in which it happened.
2. **The chamber.** The Harbourmaster certifies her chamber's tallies and her chamber's
   hurts and sends them up; she may certify nothing she has not seen counted.
3. **The Quartermaster-General.** She counts the counted, holds the parley book and the
   hands' complaints, and reports the whole — the hurts first — to the Raad at every
   sitting and to De Heeren Vijf at every quarter.
4. **The Raad van Kapiteins.** It sets the marks for the year, decides what shall be done
   about them, and answers for the doing.
5. **De Heeren Vijf.** They sit as the Muster, hear the Advocaat-Boekhouder's figures and
   the Quartermaster-General alone, and approve the reckonin' o' the year. Without their
   approval nothing is published.
6. **The Prize Council.** Every refusal to serve a commission, and every restitution, goes
   to it, and from it into the conduct disclosures.

## 8. Who signs the reckonin' o' the year

The sustainability statement for FY2025 (Salacia period key 102024-092025, the Kermis year
ended 2025-09-30) is signed by four hands:

| signatory | office | capacity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Captain-General | presiding officer of the Raad van Kapiteins | signs for the management tier |
| Presiding Lord of De Heeren Vijf | presiding officer of the supervisory tier | signs for the supervisory tier |
| Quartermaster-General | elected by all hands | countersignature |
| Advocaat-Boekhouder | secretary and chief bookkeeper | attestation that the figures agree with the ledgers |

The four are named in [the officers' register](people.md).

## 9. What the officers be paid

The Gilde pays no bonus, holds no options and ties no cash to a mark. Officers are paid as
the hands are paid: a share on the ladder the articles set — 2 shares for a captain,
1.5 for the Quartermaster-General, 1.25 for an officer, 1 for a hand — on top of the
victuallin' and lodging floor every enrolled hand is guaranteed. That is the whole of the
Gilde's variable pay, and it is described in
[the articles](articles-of-agreement.md).

One string is attached, and it pulls the other way from a bonus: a fifth of every officer's
share for the year stays in the common chest until the Quartermaster-General has counted
the year's hurts and the year's tally and De Heeren Vijf have approved the reckonin'. If
the count is not delivered, or is delivered false, the fifth is not released, and the
Quartermaster-General's veto holds it there. We report this plainly because it is the only
link between an officer's purse and the Gilde's sustainability performance, and an auditor
looking for a bonus scheme will otherwise look in vain.
